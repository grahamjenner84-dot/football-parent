## 1. Fact Check Issues

**1.1 TCA CarbonForce Pro shorts: the listing only covers one child size** (High confidence)
- Original: `{"label":"Under the shorts","name":"TCA CarbonForce Pro compression shorts, boys", ... "note":"Shorter compression-style shorts for players who want a layer without full-length tights. Same colour rule as tights."}`
- Problem: on 2026-10-13 the linked listing B08DG7J3Z8 had one size variation, **"6-8 Years"**, in two colours: "Black Stealth" and "Sonic Yellow". It is presented as a general undershorts pick, but it fits nobody over about 8, and the yellow option is no use for most clubs' match colours. A parent of a 10 to 14-year-old who clicks through finds nothing in their child's size.
- Fix: replace it with a listing that covers ages 6 to 14 or so in black, white and navy (a PUMA LIGA or Nike Park undershort, or a TCA listing with the full size run). Run the `football-parent-affiliate-link` skill to get the tagged URL. If you keep this listing for now, change the note so it is honest:
  - Replace with: `"note":"Shorter compression-style shorts for players who want a layer without full-length tights. This listing is sized for about 6 to 8 years, in black. Same colour rule as tights."}`
- Source: Amazon listing https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B08DG7J3Z8 (variation data fetched 2026-10-13).

**1.2 Sizing section says Joma sizes by age. It does not.** (High confidence)
- Original: "**By age in years** (for example 7-8 or 9-10). Common with PUMA, Joma and many cheaper sets."
- Problem: the article contradicts itself. The quick picks correctly say Joma comes in "6XS up to XS". The Joma Brama Academy listing's kids' sizes are 6XS-5XS, 4XS-3XS and XXS-XS. That is Joma's own letter code, not ages. PUMA is sized both by age (5-6 to 16 Years) and by height in cm (116 to 176) on the same listings.
- Replace with:
  ```
  - **By age in years** (for example 7-8 or 9-10). Common with PUMA and many cheaper sets.
  - **By youth letter size** (for example YXS to YXL, or youth S to XL). Under Armour and Nike teamwear size this way, set against height and chest rather than age.
  - **By Joma's own XS codes.** Joma's kids' sizes run 6XS-5XS, 4XS-3XS and XXS-XS. The chart on the listing converts them to age and height, and 6XS-5XS is the smallest.
  - **By height in centimetres** (116, 128, 140 and so on). PUMA labels often carry this next to the age.
  ```
- Source: https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B07G9FGVV5 and https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B077SPDQX5 (variation data fetched 2026-10-13).

## 3. Oversimplified or Incomplete Explanations

**3.1 "Rolling subs in mini-soccer"**
- Current: "Rolling subs in mini-soccer mean some children are on and off all game."
- Nuance: roll-on, roll-off substitutes are used across youth football, not only in mini-soccer. That makes the point stronger for older age groups as well.
- Replace with: "Rolling substitutions in youth football mean some children are on and off all game."

**3.2 "How many to buy" logic is muddled**
- Current: "For most children, two base layer tops cover a season: one in the club's sleeve colour for matches, and one in any colour for training. Add a second match colour if your child trains more than twice a week, so one is always dry."
- Problem: the extra top that helps a child who trains a lot is a second training top, not a second match-colour one. As written, a parent cannot tell why.
- Replace with: "For most children, two base layer tops cover a season: one in the club's sleeve colour for matches, and one in any colour for training. If your child trains more than twice a week, a second training top means one is always dry. The match one can double up for training at a push, but not the other way round."

**3.3 Radiator drying**
- Current: "Dry on a radiator or airer rather than a hot tumble dryer, which can damage the stretch fibres."
- Nuance: direct high heat on a hot radiator does the same thing to elastane as a tumble dryer.
- Replace with: "Dry on an airer, or near a radiator rather than draped over a hot one, and skip the tumble dryer: high heat damages the stretch fibres."

**3.4 Head covers paragraph: "a plain black sports beanie is usually fine"**
- Accurate, but the article misses the team condition. Law 4 says head covers must be black or the main shirt colour, "provided that the players of the same team wear the same colour". The article mentions this in brackets. It is fine as is, but the paragraph is 500 characters (see 7.4).

- **The colour rule examples are the most useful thing on the page.** Keep them. Add one more for a common real case: a striped or hooped shirt. Suggested addition after the third example bullet:

  (Supported by the Law 4 wording: "main colour of the shirt sleeve".)
- **Sizing measurement advice is vague.** "Measure your child's height and chest" could say how: chest round the fullest part under the armpits, height without shoes. That is one short sentence and it reduces returns.
