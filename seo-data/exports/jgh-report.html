Advertising due diligence

Sizing Up Junior Grassroots Hub

How big Junior Grassroots Hub actually is next to footballparent.co.uk, what they rank for, and whether AI assistants know they exist. TeamStats measured alongside for reference.

11 September 2026 · revised DataForSEO Labs · Google SERP · Search Console API spend $1.26

The short answer

They are bigger than us, but nothing like 64× bigger, which is what a naive tool comparison would tell you. On real Search Console numbers from both sides, Junior Grassroots Hub gets roughly 5× our impressions and somewhere between 10× and 20× our clicks.

What you would actually be buying

Almost none of their search traffic touches the pages a partner listing lives on. 88% of it lands on league tables and club directory pages, and the /partners, /supporters, /guides and /coaches sections together pull about 2%. A listing there is close to invisible in search.

That does not make it worthless, it changes what it is. The value is their social audience (115K Facebook, 57K Instagram, self-reported) and a link from a 65-referring-domain site with a cleaner backlink profile than ours. I checked the raw HTML of a live partner page: the outbound link carries rel="noopener noreferrer" and no nofollow or sponsored, so it is followed today. Read the caveat on that below before pricing it in.

Judge it as a mentions-and-social buy. That framing matters more than it sounds: the research below shows the thing actually holding us back with AI assistants is third-party mentions, and a partner listing is exactly that kind of page.

Why the 64× figure is wrong

DataForSEO credits us with 355 clicks a month. Search Console says we got 2,135 clicks in the last 28 days, about 2,290 a month. The tool is undercounting us by roughly 6.5×, and the reason is visible in the query counts: GSC records 3,874 distinct queries bringing us impressions, and DataForSEO's index knows about 157 of them.

That is what a young, long-tail informational site looks like to a third-party keyword tool. Junior Grassroots Hub has the opposite shape. Their traffic sits on a few hundred high-volume, well-indexed league names, which these tools cover accurately, and their own published figures line up with the estimate within about 2×.

The claims look honest rather than inflated, but treat them as their numbers, not measured ones.

Where their traffic actually lands

This is the part that matters for an advertising decision. Their top 1,000 keywords account for about 78% of their estimated traffic, and it is overwhelmingly directory lookups.

The visitor profile that follows: a parent or coach checking a fixture or a league table, mid-season, probably on a phone, probably a repeat visit to the same page. High intent for that page, very low browsing intent. They are not there to read anything.

TeamStats is the same business model, more so: /league pages are 85.3% of their traffic, and their blog is 0.8%.

What that argues for

Not four new category pages. That was my earlier advice and the data does not support it: we would be splitting authority away from a page that already ranks #1 for those queries, and building for a cluster worth ~120 impressions a quarter.

The cheaper and lower-risk move is to add the club lists to the page Google already ranks. It is purely additive, it does not touch the URL or the existing headings, and it serves an intent we are demonstrably already being shown for and failing to convert. The raw material partly exists on premier-league-development-centres-list, though that covers only Premier League clubs, and Category 1 also includes EFL academies such as Southampton, Norwich, Stoke, Sunderland and Nottingham Forest.

Caveat worth stating plainly: this page is our single biggest click earner at 253 clicks in 28 days and is climbing. Any edit is a live-traffic edit, so it should be one additive change, its own commit, and then left alone on the 10 to 14 day watch.

Development centres are a different question

You asked whether the academy traffic is really development-centre traffic. Partly, and it splits cleanly in two.

  • Club-specific development centre queries are ours. "chelsea development centre": we are #3 organic and cited #4 in the AI Overview. "arsenal development centre": #5 organic, cited #4 in the AI Overview. Junior Grassroots Hub does not appear in the top 20 for either. They are not competing with us here at all.
  • Generic development centre queries are nobody's, and not ours. "football development centres" is owned end to end by club community trusts — Brentford, Fulham, Ipswich, Peterborough, Arsenal, Birmingham. We are not in the top 20. The AI Overview on that query cites no sources at all.

Those unattributed AI Overviews came up repeatedly: "football development centres", "development centre football", "football academy categories explained" and "academies near me" all generate an answer box citing nothing. There is no citation to win on those queries, by anyone.

Is Category 3 the opportunity? No

The reasoning was: we sit at 2.0 for "cat 1 academy list" and 11.7 for "cat 3 academies list", so pushing Category 3 up should multiply its impressions. Four things in the data say otherwise.

  • There is no Category 3 weakness. Grouping every category query by the number it names, impression-weighted positions are 7.5 for Cat 1, 7.8 for Cat 2, 8.5 for Cat 3 and 6.2 for Cat 4. Flat. Cat 4 is our strongest, not Cat 1.
  • The impression logic runs the other way. "cat 3 academies list" took 14 impressions at position 11.7; "cat 1 academy list" took 12 at position 1.0. More impressions from the worse position. Those two rows were long-tail noise, not a pattern.
  • Cat 3 already out-earns Cat 2 and Cat 4. 345 impressions over 90 days against 197 and 204.
  • One page ranks for all of it. academy-categories-explained serves every category query, so there is no separate Cat 3 page to improve. It is the same lever whichever category you aim at.

And it is fixing itself

Across the whole category cluster, comparing the last 28 days against the 28 before: weighted position 9.43 to 6.89, impressions 494 to 906, clicks 8 to 19, CTR 1.62% to 2.10%. Every Cat 3 query improved by 2.3 to 3.9 places in a single month, unaided.

This is the case the change guardrails exist for. Editing a page mid-climb makes the climb unreadable, and if the metric then dips you cannot tell whether it was you. Leave it and re-measure in two to three weeks.

How to actually get picked up by AI

The two surfaces reward completely different things, and conflating them is why "AI optimisation" advice is usually useless. What the data here supports:

For Google AI Overviews: rank top five, nothing else moves it

Across all 15 checks, every editorial source in every AI Overview came from the organic top eight, and almost all from the top five. We are cited on every academy and development-centre query where we rank top five, and on none where we do not. "best shin pads for kids" at #13 is the clearest case.

  • There is no separate AI lever here. Ranking work is the AI work. The queries worth targeting are the ones where we are #6–13 with an AI Overview present, because moving up a few places flips the citation on.
  • Do not chase unattributed answer boxes. Four of the queries generate an AI Overview citing nobody. There is no prize on those.

For ChatGPT: rank is close to irrelevant, mentions are everything

This is the part worth internalising. A 40-keyword site got recommended twice for a topic we rank #1 on. Assistant answers are assembled from what the open web says about you, not from where you sit in Google.

  • Get named on the pages assistants actually retrieve. In our tests those were: governing bodies (England Football, The FA, county FAs), established magazines (FourFourTwo), Reddit threads, and roundup or directory pages of the "best websites for football parents" kind. Being listed on those pages is the mechanism, not outranking them.
  • Concretely, that means: getting into "best UK football parent websites" roundups; answering in the Reddit and Facebook grassroots parenting threads that appeared in these SERPs; a county FA or league site linking to a guide; and being listed in directories. A Junior Grassroots Hub partner listing is literally one of these pages, which is the strongest argument in its favour.
  • Make the brand name unambiguous in your own copy. Every page should state "Football Parent (footballparent.co.uk)" somewhere an extractor will read it, so an assistant summarising us has the domain attached to the name and cannot merge us with the lookalike.
  • Give assistants a thing to point at per question. Both sites that beat us have one URL per narrow intent. That helps in ChatGPT for the same reason it helps in Google: a page whose whole subject is the question is easier to cite than a section inside a longer page.

The one content change the data actually supports

Add named club lists per category to the existing explainer, rather than as new pages. See the corrected reasoning above: we already rank #1 for the list queries and convert almost none of them, so this is a conversion fix on a page that is already winning the ranking, not a new-page play.

It is also the only gap that shows up on all three surfaces at once: the list queries we rank for and do not convert, the AI Overviews on those same queries where Junior Grassroots Hub is cited and we are not, and the ChatGPT citations that went to a 40-keyword site with a club list.

Sized honestly, the direct search prize is small, about 120 impressions a quarter. The reason to do it anyway is the AI Overview citation on the list queries and the credibility of being the page that actually answers, not the traffic.

But not yet. The cluster is mid-climb, +2.5 positions in a month. See the headroom section: this edit should wait two to three weeks so the climb stays readable, and it ranks seventh on the site's opportunity list regardless.

Reading this as an advertising decision

What is genuinely good about them

  • The audience is exactly ours. UK grassroots junior football parents and coaches, no overlap wasted on adult Sunday league the way TeamStats has.
  • The link is followed, today. I pulled the raw HTML of their Precision Pass partner page: the outbound link is rel="noopener noreferrer", with no nofollow and no sponsored. Their profile is 65 referring domains at spam score 14, against our 10 domains at spam score 56.
  • A partner listing is the kind of page AI assistants retrieve. On the evidence above, that may be worth more than the link.
  • They are growing. 6,569 keywords gained against 2,218 lost, and 17 first-place rankings.
  • Entry price is low. £99 for three months. Small enough to treat as a test rather than a commitment.

The caveat on that followed link

An unmarked paid link is contrary to Google's own link-spam guidance, which says paid placements should carry rel="sponsored" or nofollow. Three consequences worth pricing in: Google's algorithms may already discount a site-wide pattern of paid partner links regardless of markup; Junior Grassroots Hub could add the attribute at any time, without telling you; and strictly speaking both sides are outside the guidelines.

So do not buy this for the link equity. Buy it for the mention and the social reach, and treat the followed link as a bonus that may not last. That is the right way round even though the link is real today.

What to be sceptical about

  • "300K+ parents, coaches and club volunteers" is a reach claim, not a traffic claim. It leans on 115K Facebook and 57K Instagram. Their search traffic is around 695K impressions a month, and impressions are not people.
  • A partner listing sits on the 2% of the site nobody arrives at. /partners takes 0.2% of their search traffic. Search will not deliver anyone to your listing; only their internal linking and their social posts will.
  • Their visitors are not in a browsing mood. Checking a league table is a ten-second, single-purpose visit. This is a poor environment for anything needing consideration, which is most of what we publish.
  • They outrank us on the academy cluster and are cited by AI on it. Not a reason to avoid them, but be clear you would be paying a site that competes with us on one topic.

What I would ask them before paying

  • Can the placement go on league pages rather than a partner directory entry? That is where 75% of their traffic is. Central Warwickshire and Tandridge alone are roughly 4,700 estimated clicks a month.
  • Is the link followed, and does it survive if you stop paying? A rel="sponsored" link is honest and fine, but it changes the SEO value to roughly zero, which changes what the £99 buys.
  • What does a partner post to 115K Facebook followers actually look like, and how many did the last few get? Social reach is the real product here, so ask for the last three partners' numbers.
  • Can you have referral numbers from an existing partner? They list Veo and XbotGo. Those are directly comparable advertisers.

The honest summary: they are a real site with a real audience about five times our size on impressions, their self-reported numbers survive a cross-check, and their link profile is cleaner than ours. But the search traffic in the pitch lands nowhere near where a partner listing sits, so it is not a traffic buy.

At £99 for three months it is cheap enough to test, and the strongest argument for it is the one that only emerged from the AI research: a partner listing is exactly the sort of third-party page assistants retrieve when a parent asks them which websites to trust. That is the gap we actually have. Ask the four questions above, and if the answers are reasonable, it is a defensible £99.
