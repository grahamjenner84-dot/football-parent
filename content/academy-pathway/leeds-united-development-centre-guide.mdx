## What Is the Leeds United Development Centre?

Parents searching for a "Leeds United development centre" are usually looking for a programme that, strictly speaking, doesn't carry that name. Leeds United Foundation's below-academy provision for boys is split across two stages called the Skills Centre and the Technical Centre, sitting separately from the club's formal, [Category One academy](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained).

That academy is based at Thorp Arch, the club's training ground near Wetherby, and Leeds have held Category One status, the top tier under the Premier League's [Elite Player Performance Plan](https://www.premierleague.com/en/footballandcommunity/youth-development/eppp), since July 2020. Before that, the club had been a Category Two academy since EPPP began in 2012, so Leeds's top-tier status is more recent than some of the historically Category One clubs also covered on this site, reflecting a specific period of investment in the academy setup under the club's current ownership rather than a status Leeds has held continuously.

This guide covers what the Skills Centre and Technical Centre actually involve, how the girls' pathway is structured, and how the whole system connects, or doesn't, to the formal academy.

## Skills Centres and Technical Centres Explained

According to [Leeds United Foundation's own football and sports information](https://www.leedsunited.com/en/foundation/football-and-sports), the Foundation runs seven weekly Skills Centres across the region, open to the public to book directly online. Sessions run for an hour and focus on individual technical development under what the club calls "the Leeds United Way", covering ball control, movement, passing and receiving, and attacking and defending. Published age ranges vary slightly by source, but Foundation materials generally put Skills Centre sessions at roughly ages 5 to 15.

Above that sits a single Technical Centre, run on an invitation-only basis rather than open booking. Technical Centre sessions run for an hour and 15 minutes and include a regular games programme alongside individual development work, term-time only. This tier is generally aimed at boys aged around 7 to 14, with a further, more advanced Under-14 to Under-16 Technical Centre group for players who progress that far. Players who show the right attributes at Technical Centre level can be referred through the pathway into the Academy itself, though the Foundation is clear this is a possibility rather than a guarantee.

Separately, Leeds United Foundation periodically runs paid Open Trials events, distinct from the Skills Centre and Technical Centre route. These have covered boys from Under-6 upward, roughly a year group at a time, held at a local venue rather than at Thorp Arch itself, with a small booking fee. The exact top age has varied between events, so check the specific age groups advertised for the trial you're looking at, and check the Foundation's own trials information rather than assuming a fixed annual event.

## The Leeds United Academy Pathway

Category One status brings Leeds United's academy increased funding compared with lower categories, along with access to a more competitive fixture programme: the Under-23s play in Premier League 2, and the Under-18s compete in the [U18 Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/youth-competitions/u18/u18-premier-league-explained) North division, with the club also eligible for the EFL Trophy and UEFA Youth League at senior level. According to [reporting at the time of the promotion](http://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/leeds-united-awarded-category-one-status-on-landmark-day), the club's leadership had targeted Category One status as a five-year goal and reached it in three.

Entry into the academy proper is far more selective than either Foundation programme, and isn't something a family applies for directly. Most players arrive through Technical Centre referral or direct scouting, and a place at a Skills Centre or Technical Centre is not a guaranteed step towards it. Our guide to [development centres vs academies](/academy-pathway/development-centres-vs-academies) covers this general distinction in more depth, and [what age football academies recruit](/academy-pathway/what-age-do-football-academies-recruit) sets out how recruitment ages work across clubs more broadly.

## Quick Answer

**What is the Leeds United Development Centre?** Leeds don't officially use that name. The closest equivalents are the Skills Centre (open to book) and the Technical Centre (invitation only), both run by Leeds United Foundation below the club's Category One academy.

**What age can my child join?** Skills Centres cover roughly ages 5 to 15, and the Technical Centre roughly 7 to 14, with a further invitation-only group for 14 to 16-year-olds. Open Trials, when running, have covered Under-6 upward a year group at a time, though the exact top age has varied by event.

**How do you get scouted or get a trial with Leeds?** Book a Skills Centre session directly, get referred into the Technical Centre through it or through grassroots/school football, or enter a periodic Open Trials event when one is advertised.

**Is the development centre the same as the main academy?** No. Both the Skills Centre and Technical Centre sit outside the formal, EPPP-registered academy. A Technical Centre place can lead to a referral, but most players who attend won't progress into the academy itself.

**Is it worth it? What does it cost?** The Foundation doesn't publish fixed fees for the Skills Centre or Technical Centre, so it's worth asking directly what a place costs and what that covers. Open Trials have carried their own separate, smaller booking fee.

## How to Get Your Child Seen by Leeds United

There's no single guaranteed route in, but the realistic paths are:

- Booking a Skills Centre session directly through the Foundation's online booking system, since these sessions are open rather than invitational
- Playing regularly at a reasonable standard of grassroots or school football in the Leeds and West Yorkshire area, since that's where most Technical Centre scouting happens
- Being referred into the Technical Centre from a Skills Centre or by a coach already working within the Foundation
- Attending an advertised Open Trials event when one is announced for your child's age group

For direct enquiries, the Foundation can be contacted at foundation@leedsunited.com or on 0113 367 6014. For more on how recruitment generally works across clubs beyond Leeds specifically, our guide to [how football clubs recruit young players](/academy-trials/how-football-clubs-recruit-young-players) is useful background.

## What Leeds United Looks for in Young Players

Foundation coaches assess players over a run of sessions rather than from a single trial. What's being assessed is broader than raw ability: technical grounding under pressure, how a child responds to coaching and correction, and how they engage with teammates all factor in, alongside physical attributes appropriate to the age group.

An invitation to a Technical Centre, or a referral from one towards the academy, is the start of an observation process rather than a sign that academy registration is close. Most boys who attend a Skills Centre or Technical Centre will stay at that level, and that's a normal outcome rather than a sign of failure. For a broader look at what coaches are watching for at this stage, see our guides on [what happens at academy trials](/academy-trials/what-happens-at-academy-trials) and [what academy coaches look for](/academy-trials/what-do-academy-coaches-look-for).

From experience of other clubs' development centre programmes, the quality of the coaching, and of the centre overall, tended to be more about the individual coaches than which club it was. Some coaches could demand high standards and still keep the kids engaged and make it fun. Others had the high standards without the same rapport with the kids. ## Does Leeds United Have a Development Centre for Girls?

Yes, and Leeds's girls' pathway is more heavily tiered than the boys' side. According to [Leeds United's own Women and Girls information](https://www.leedsunited.com/en/foundation/women-and-girls), the structure runs, broadly youngest to most advanced:

- **Girls Only Football Sessions:** non-competitive introductory sessions for primary schools and grassroots clubs, focused on general movement skills alongside football
- **Girls Technical Centres (roughly ages 5 to 16):** described by the club as the first step into the Female Pathway, run in 12-week blocks across seven locations, with players kept eligible to keep playing for their grassroots club at the same time
- **Emerging Talent Centre (Under-10 to Under-12):** an FA-sanctioned programme intended to widen access to the pathway
- **Performance Centre of Excellence (roughly Under-7 to Under-16):** invitation-only, reached through a Technical Centre referral
- **Girls Academy (Under-14 to Under-16):** the most selective stage, with players training multiple times a week and access to Leeds Beckett University's facilities alongside strength and conditioning, nutrition and psychological support
- **Post-16 Education Programme:** a dual football and study route run in partnership with Leeds City College

The Foundation's own published figures for 2024/25 put 55% of players who attended exclusive Technical Centre trials going on to join either the Performance Centre of Excellence or the Emerging Talent Centre, and 60% of players accepted into the Girls Academy that year came through the Performance Centre of Excellence route specifically. If you're weighing Leeds's girls' setup against the wider landscape, our guide to [how girls' football academies work in the UK](/girls-football/how-girls-football-academies-work) covers how the current system fits together nationally.

## Safeguarding and Parent Checks

Any programme involving regular sessions with your child, whether it's a Skills Centre, the Technical Centre, an Open Trials event or the formal academy, is worth a few practical checks before you commit:

- Ask who the designated safeguarding lead is for the specific programme, and how to raise a concern
- Ask how coaches are vetted (DBS checks and relevant FA coaching qualifications)
- Check the programme's policy on communication with children outside sessions, and on photos, video and social media
- Be cautious of any third party unconnected to the club charging for a "Leeds United trial", and confirm directly with the Foundation that an event is genuinely theirs before paying anything
- Prioritise a setting where your child feels safe and heard over any promise of a pathway to the academy

[NSPCC's Child Protection in Sport Unit](https://sport.nspcc.org.uk/) is a good general resource for a wider checklist to work through before your child joins any club programme.

## Is It Worth It? An Honest Parent's View

For a child who enjoys structured coaching and wants a step up from grassroots training, a Skills Centre or Technical Centre place can be genuinely worthwhile on its own terms, regardless of whether academy interest ever follows. As covered above, the individual coach your child gets tends to shape that experience more than anything else about the programme.

Thorp Arch itself sits out at Wetherby, some way from central Leeds, though Skills Centre and Technical Centre sessions run across several more local venues rather than only at the training ground, so it's worth checking exactly where your specific age group trains before assuming a long drive is involved. Weighing the travel and time commitment against what your child would get from a similar standard of coaching closer to home is a reasonable question to ask, whichever club you're considering.

## FAQs

### How do you get into the Leeds United Academy?

There's no direct application route into the academy itself. Most players arrive through a Technical Centre referral after progressing from a Skills Centre, through direct scouting at grassroots or school level, or occasionally via an Open Trials event, though none of these guarantee an academy place.

### What age does the Leeds United Academy start recruiting?

Leeds's own Foundation programmes start from around age 5 at Skills Centre level, but that's a long way from formal academy registration. For how recruitment ages generally work at Category One academies across English football, see our guide to [what age football academies recruit](/academy-pathway/what-age-do-football-academies-recruit).

### How does Leeds United's academy compare to other Category One academies?

Category One is a threshold, not a ranking. It sets a minimum standard for facilities, coaching provision and player welfare that every club at that level, from Leeds to clubs that have held the status for decades, has to meet under EPPP. Leeds reached that threshold more recently than some clubs, having been promoted from Category Two in 2020, but "Category One" itself doesn't rank clubs against one another.

### Do Leeds United academy players get paid?

From around 16, players who progress onto a two-year scholarship agreement do get paid, though [the amount varies by academy category and budget rather than being a fixed rate](https://thefootballscholar.com/who-is-a-football-scholar/). We couldn't find a reliable published source specifically covering pay for players below scholarship age, so we're not stating a figure either way. It's worth asking any club directly what, if anything, applies to your child's specific age group.

"Leeds United development centre" covers two Foundation programmes that don't actually carry that name: the open-booking Skills Centre and the invitation-only Technical Centre, both separate from the club's Category One academy at Thorp Arch. The girls' pathway runs a more heavily tiered structure, from introductory sessions through to the Girls Academy. Treat any invitation, on either side of the club, as the start of an observation process rather than a guaranteed step towards the academy.
