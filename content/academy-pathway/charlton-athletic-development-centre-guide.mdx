## How Youth Football at Charlton Is Organised

Charlton Athletic's youth football splits into three parts, and a family searching for "Charlton development centre" can land in any of them:

- **The Charlton Athletic Academy**, a Category Two academy at the club's training ground in New Eltham, which signs boys through scouting
- **Charlton Athletic Community Trust (CACT)**, the club's charity, which runs paid Development Centres for ages 9 to 16 and an invitation-only Advanced Centre
- **Charlton Athletic Women and Girls**, with its own Girls Academy, an FA Emerging Talent Centre and a girls' Advanced Centre

The academy and the Trust share a postcode, and the Trust's best players can be passed to the academy. A Trust place on its own doesn't register a child with the academy, though, and the two are run by different staff.

South-east London is crowded academy country. Crystal Palace, Millwall and Charlton all scout the same pool of players, and a strong Under-10 in Bexley or Greenwich can be watched by all three. For the Palace, Millwall and other London options side by side, see our list of [football development centres in London](/academy-pathway/football-development-centres-in-london).

## The Charlton Academy at Sparrows Lane

The academy trains at Sparrows Lane in New Eltham, SE9. The club describes it as [home to every representative team](https://www.cafc.co.uk/sparrows-lane-training-ground), from the academy's Foundation Phase through the Under-18s and Under-21s to the men's and women's first teams. Two of its pitches were rebuilt ahead of the 2024/25 season.

Charlton's academy is Category Two under the Elite Player Performance Plan, one grade below the top grade, Category One. The full [list of Category Two academies for 2026/27](/academy-pathway/football-academies-by-category-list) shows the 16 other clubs in the same grade.

That grade sets a travel limit our guide to [academy categories](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained) explains: Under-9 to Under-11 players must live within an hour of the training ground, and Under-12 to Under-16 players within 90 minutes. For Charlton the hour is measured from Sparrows Lane in New Eltham, a few miles south-east of The Valley.

The grade undersells its record. Training Ground Guru's productivity rankings, which count each academy's graduates playing league football, had Charlton [the third highest Category Two academy in 2020/21](http://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/pell-appointed-charlton-academy-manager-with-avory-moving-to-new-role), behind only Crewe and Sheffield United.

The club says [more than 50 of its graduates](https://www.cafc.co.uk/academy), aged 16 to 20, have played for the men's first team since 2013/14, including Joe Gomez, Ezri Konsa, Ademola Lookman and Nick Pope.

For a parent, that record is the main reason to take a Charlton approach seriously: teenagers there do get first-team chances.

## Are There Charlton Academy Trials?

No, not for boys. Charlton hasn't advertised a public academy trial day for boys in 2025 or 2026, and its academy pages describe no trial process.

Players reach the academy the way most do. A scout sees them playing for a grassroots, school or Trust team, and the academy invites them in for a period of training. The steps from a scout's first visit to a training invitation are in our guide to [how football clubs recruit young players](/academy-trials/how-football-clubs-recruit-young-players).

The club does publish two addresses on its [contact page](https://www.cafc.co.uk/get-in-touch): academy@cafc.co.uk "for information on the academy and trials", and scouting@cafc.co.uk for scouting requests. Emailing is worth it if your child plays at a good level and you want to know whether a scout covers their league. Treat it as that question. The usual route in is still a scout watching your child play.

Picture a Year 5 player in a strong Kent or south London league. Being seen there, by a Charlton, Palace or Millwall scout, is the most likely route into any of the three academies. Anyone charging money for a "Charlton academy trial" isn't acting for the academy.

## CACT Development Centres

Charlton Athletic Community Trust runs the paid side of youth football at the club. Its [Development Centres](https://www.cafc.co.uk/cact/what-we-do/football-and-sports-development/cact-development-centres) are for boys and girls aged 9 to 16 who already play club football. Each block lasts 12 weeks, with UEFA and FA licensed coaches, covering 7, 9 and 11-a-side football and a separate goalkeeper group.

The autumn 2026 block ran from 14 September to 7 December. The [Trust's booking site](https://cact.gives/courses) listed centres at:

- **Charlton Training Ground**, entered via Footscray Rugby Club on Footscray Road, Eltham: groups for 9 to 11, 9 to 12 and 13 to 16 year olds
- **Sutcliffe Park Sports Centre**, Eltham Road: 9 to 11 year olds, Mondays
- **Bexley Park**, Dartford: 9 to 11 year olds, Tuesdays
- **Sporting Club Thamesmead**: 9 to 16 year olds, Mondays

Sessions were £8.50 each, about £100 for a 12-week block. That's low for a professional club's programme: [QPR's Trust Showcase squad](/academy-pathway/qpr-development-centre-guide), for comparison, costs £80 a month. Goalkeeper Development Centres for ages 6 to 10 and 11 to 15 run at the training ground at the same price.

For younger children the Trust runs Charlton Challenge, for ages two to eight, at £7.50 a session in Greenwich and Bexley. The Trust treats it as the step before the Development Centres, which start at nine.

Take a Year 5 boy from Eltham who plays for his grassroots team on Sundays and trains with them on Wednesdays. A Monday Development Centre adds a third session at a low price. On its own it doesn't put him in front of the academy. The Trust does pick some players from these groups for its Advanced Centre.

## The Advanced Centre

The [Advanced Centre](https://www.cafc.co.uk/cact/what-we-do/football-and-sports-development/advanced-centre) is the Trust's selected group, and the part of its work closest to the academy. It's invitation only: places go to players recruited by Charlton or to those who have shown potential on one of the Trust's own programmes, including holiday courses, Charlton Challenge and the Development Centres.

Sessions are at the training ground with UEFA A and B licensed coaches, and players have fixtures against local academies and Charlton's own academy teams. From there, the Trust says, a player can move into Junior Premier League football, stay in the Advanced Centre, or be invited to a trial with the academy.

That last route is the one parents ask about. It exists, but the Trust doesn't say how many Advanced Centre players are invited to an academy trial each season, so ask before you assume it's likely. The Trust's page describes the boys' Advanced Centre as covering U5 to U13, while its Development Centres run to 16, so check which age groups are taking players this season. The boys' and girls' groups run separately.

If your child moves into a Junior Premier League team instead, our guide to [the JPL](/parent-guides/what-is-the-junior-premier-league) explains how that league works and what it costs families.

## Girls' Football at Charlton

Charlton runs a full girls' pathway, and it's more open to approaches than the boys' academy.

- **Girls Academy.** When the FA ended its Regional Talent Clubs in 2023, Charlton [renamed its youth section the Girls Academy](https://www.cafc.co.uk/teams/womens-academy). Its Under-14 to Under-16 sides play in the JPL Warriors Elite divisions, and its Under-12 and Under-13 sides in the Tandridge Youth League.
- **Emerging Talent Centre.** Charlton's FA [Emerging Talent Centre](/girls-football/emerging-talent-centres-explained) is free, for girls at Under-9 to Under-12, and trains on Tuesdays from 5pm to 6.30pm on the Trust's 3G pitch next to the training ground. Girls register an interest, then some are invited to an open session before each new block. Home must be within 60 minutes of the training ground.
- **Girls' Advanced Centre.** The Trust's girls' sessions run on Monday evenings at the training ground for Under-9 to Under-16, at £8 a session, with a goalkeeper group. They are for girls who already play competitively, and the Trust says they lead into the JPL, its post-16 programme, the ETC and the Girls Academy.
- **Girls Play.** Recreational sessions for girls aged 3 to 11, in Thamesmead and Bexleyheath.

Our guide to [girls' academy trials by club](/girls-football/girls-academy-trials-by-club) has Charlton's current ETC dates beside the other London clubs'.

For a girl in Year 5 who already plays for a grassroots team, the Girls' Advanced Centre is a sensible first step: it costs £8 a session, it runs alongside her Saturday or Sunday football, and the Trust names the ETC and the Girls Academy among its next steps.

## What to Look For Before You Book

Children on Trust programmes keep playing for their grassroots clubs, so judge a programme on the coaching your child gets each week.

<ParentNote>
The main thing I'd do is ask other parents about the pathway and about the centre itself: how many players have progressed through the foundation to the academy, and what the process is for that to happen. Knowing what you want to get from it, and asking those questions up front, is probably the best thing you can do.
</ParentNote>

Questions to ask before you commit to a block:

- How many players from this group have moved to the Advanced Centre, or been looked at by an academy, in the last two seasons?
- Will my child have the same coach for the whole block?
- How big are the groups, and how much of each session is spent on the ball?
- Is there any feedback during the block, written or spoken?

If you can, watch a session before you pay for the block. A coach who stops to fix a poor first touch, shows the skill well and keeps a group of ten-year-olds listening is a better sign than the venue or the kit.

A Trust centre and an academy are different things, and our comparison of [development centres and academies](/academy-pathway/development-centres-vs-academies) sets out where each one leads.

## Safeguarding and Parent Checks

The same checks apply to a CACT block, a girls' ETC session or an approach from the academy:

- Find out who the welfare officer or safeguarding lead is, and how you would report a worry
- Ask whether every coach has an enhanced DBS check and an FA coaching qualification
- Ask whether coaches ever message children directly, and what the rules are on photos and video
- If someone at a grassroots game says they scout for Charlton, check with the club at scouting@cafc.co.uk before passing on your child's details

[England Football's safeguarding guidance](https://www.englandfootball.com/participate/behaviour/safeguarding) sets out what every club and programme should have in place.

## FAQ: Charlton Academy and Trials

### What category is the Charlton academy?

Category Two under the Elite Player Performance Plan. It has one of the strongest records in that grade for producing first-team players.

---

### Where does the Charlton academy train?

At Sparrows Lane in New Eltham, SE9 2JR, which it shares with the first teams. The Trust's sessions at the training ground are entered via Footscray Rugby Club on Footscray Road.

---

### How much do Charlton Development Centres cost?

In autumn 2026 the Trust charged £8.50 a session for its Development Centres for ages 9 to 16, run in 12-week blocks. Charlton Challenge for ages 2 to 8 was £7.50 a session.

---

### Can my child play for a Charlton Trust programme and their grassroots team?

Yes. The Development Centres are run by the Trust, so your child stays registered with their grassroots club. The girls' ETC is built the same way.

---

### How do I contact Charlton about academy trials?

The club lists academy@cafc.co.uk for academy and trial information and scouting@cafc.co.uk for scouting. For Trust programmes, book through cact.gives or call the Trust on 020 8850 2866.

## Summary

Charlton's academy is Category Two, trains at Sparrows Lane in New Eltham and has one of the best records in its grade for turning boys into first-team players. There are no open trials for boys: the academy finds them through its scouts.

The Trust's Development Centres cost £8.50 a session for ages 9 to 16, and its invitation-only Advanced Centre is the Trust's link to the academy. Girls have more open doors: a free ETC that girls can register an interest in, a Monday Advanced Centre, and a Girls Academy above both.

For most families the useful question is whether the coaching is worth another evening a week, which a block at £8.50 a session is a low-risk way to find out.
