## How Youth Football at QPR Is Organised

Queens Park Rangers have no open "development centre" in the sense Chelsea or Wolves use the phrase. A family looking for a weekly QPR session to book will find three separate things instead:

- **The QPR Academy**, a Category Two academy based at the club's training ground in Heston, which signs boys mainly through scouting
- **QPR Women and Girls**, the club's own girls' teams from Under-10 upwards, which do hold trials
- **QPR in the Community Trust**, the club's charity, which runs paid programmes including an Advanced Development Programme (ADP) and a Showcase squad

The three are run by different staff, and a place in one doesn't register a child with another. QPR play in the Championship, in one of the most crowded areas in the country for academy football: Chelsea, Fulham and Brentford all sit within a few miles of Loftus Road. Our guide to [football development centres in London](/academy-pathway/football-development-centres-in-london) sets out what the other clubs offer.

## The QPR Academy at Heston

The academy is based at the club's own site in Heston, beside the M4 in Hounslow. It was already training there before the club rebuilt the 27-acre site, and since June 2023 the first team has shared it too.

[The training ground cost about £20 million](https://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/qpr-open-state-of-the-art-training-ground,-ending-11-year-saga), with seven pitches, five of them hybrid grass with undersoil heating, a gym and a hydropool. It is now called the TSG Training Centre.

Opening it ended more than a decade of the club trying to build its own training ground. For a family in Ealing, Hounslow or Hillingdon the academy is a short drive. For a family in Shepherd's Bush or White City, near the stadium, it is a trip out west in rush hour on school nights.

The academy runs from Under-9 to Under-18, with a Development Squad above that playing Under-21 sides from other clubs. 

In 2021 academy director Alex Carroll [described a pre-academy group of under-7s and under-8s](https://www.westlondonsport.com/qpr/qpr-academy-chief-believes-club-can-become-first-pick-for-young-talent) training with the club before players could sign at Under-9. In the same interview he said it was "very much more about the software than the hardware", meaning the coaches more than the buildings.

Category Two status sets limits on who the academy can register. Under the travel rules our guide to [academy categories](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained) explains, a Category Two club can register Under-9 to Under-11 players who live within an hour of the training ground, and Under-12 to Under-16 players within 90 minutes. For QPR that means Heston, not Loftus Road, is the point the clock starts from.

## The Category One Application

In January 2026 the club [confirmed it intends to apply for Category One](https://www.qpr.co.uk/news/2026/january/14/academy-update/), the top grade under the Elite Player Performance Plan. QPR have been Category Two since the system began. Chief executive Christian Nourry said the next 18 months would be a major undertaking for the academy staff, and the statement was clear that no club is guaranteed an upgrade.

The club listed what it wants from the higher grade, and two of the four points matter directly to families:

- **A wider recruitment area.** Category One academies are not held to the 90-minute travel limit for older age groups, so QPR could sign players from further afield.
- **Higher compensation.** A Category One club receives a bigger fee when one of its academy players leaves for another club.

The others were entry to the top national youth competitions, such as Premier League 2, and the standing a top grade carries with parents of the most sought-after young players.

For a child at grassroots level now, the application changes very little this season. If it succeeds, the academy will be competing for players over a larger area, and against the same Category One neighbours it already loses some players to. If your child is approached in the meantime, the academy's current grade and travel limits are what apply.

## Are There QPR Academy Trials?

Not open ones for boys, as far as we can find. The academy has not advertised a public trial day for boys in 2025 or 2026. Players reach it the way most academy players do: a scout sees them playing for a grassroots, school or development team, and the academy invites them in for a period of training to take a longer look.

Our guide to [how football clubs recruit young players](/academy-trials/how-football-clubs-recruit-young-players) walks through that process.

Several things carry the QPR name and the word "trial" without being academy trials:

- **Girls' trials** for QPR Women and Girls, which are genuine and covered below
- **College programmes**, where a sixth-form or college football course run with QPR coaches holds trials for 16 to 18 year olds alongside studies
- **Trust programmes**, where places on the Community Trust's selected groups are filled by the Trust's own coaches

The practical example is a Year 5 boy who plays for a strong Sunday league side in west London. His most likely route to being seen by QPR is playing well in that league, where academy scouts from several clubs watch. A paid programme with QPR's crest on it might add coaching. It doesn't put him on the academy's books, and anyone charging for a "QPR academy trial" isn't acting for the academy.

## QPR in the Community Trust Programmes

QPR in the Community Trust is the club's charity, founded in 1994. It works across seven London boroughs and says it reaches around 23,000 participants a year. Its football programmes are booked through [the Trust's booking page](https://www.abler.io/shop/qpr_community_trust). In November 2026 it listed:

- **Future Hoops**, sessions for children aged roughly three to five, from £12.50
- **FA Wildcats**, girls-only sessions for ages five to 11 at Powerleague Shepherd's Bush, £4 a session
- **After-school clubs** at partner primary schools
- **Para-football**, including Tiger Cubs sessions for children aged six and over
- **The Advanced Development Programme Showcase squad**, covered below

### The Advanced Development Programme

The ADP is the Trust's selected group, for players its coaches rate above a typical community session.

The Trust doesn't publish an ADP page, but [a Trust coaching advert from October 2026](https://jobsinfootball.com/job/134787/sessional-football-coach-advanced-development-programme-adp-qpr-community-trust/) lists weekly ADP evening sessions in Willesden, Greenford, Chiswick and Shepherd's Bush, with players monitored through the season and able to move up into a Performance Development Programme. 

The ADP sessions themselves aren't on the public booking page, so ask the Trust at community@qpr.co.uk how players are picked and what each session costs.

### The Showcase squad

The Showcase is the part of the ADP you can see priced. In 2026/27 it costs £80 a month from October to May, for a minimum of eight months, plus £180 for league registration and a full playing kit. 

Training is on Friday evenings from 5.30pm to 7.30pm at Powerleague Watford in Bushey. League fixtures are at Rayners Lane or Islip Manor High School, with a minimum of 30 matches across the season, about two thirds of them from the league and the rest arranged by the programme.

That adds up to £820 for the season. The listing doesn't give an age range, so check which age groups it covers before you look further.

The Trust is a charity, and its programmes are run separately from the academy at Heston. A Showcase or ADP player is a Trust player. ## Girls' Football at QPR

The girls' side is where QPR is most open to new players. QPR Women and Girls run squads at Under-10, Under-12, Under-14 and Under-16, plus a reserve side, and fill them through trials announced on the club website.

The pattern in 2026 was:

- **May:** [trials for Under-10 to Under-16 and the reserves](https://www.qpr.co.uk/news/2026/april/30/qpr-fc-women-girls-trials/), two evenings for each group, with registration closing in early May
- **July:** a second round of Under-10 trials for girls in Years 4 and 5, registration by 2 July
- **September:** a goalkeeper talent identification session with the club's senior staff and players

The club warns that high demand means not every girl who registers is invited to trial, though some are contacted later.

At Under-10, QPR [restructured its girls' set-up in 2025 as a Player Development Centre](https://www.qpr.co.uk/news/2025/july/11/extra-dates-confirmed-for-u10s-girls-trials/): girls dual-register, staying with their grassroots team while getting an extra evening of coaching at QPR, with occasional fixtures against other elite sides on a four to six week cycle.

For a girl in Year 4 that is a light commitment that keeps her Saturday or Sunday team intact.

We couldn't find an FA Emerging Talent Centre licence held by QPR. The FA's [Emerging Talent Centres](/girls-football/emerging-talent-centres-explained) are run by other clubs in London, so a girl in west London could be watched for one of those as well. Enquiries about QPR's girls' teams go to qprfcwg@qpr.co.uk.

## What to Look For in Any Programme

Many children who do a club programme, at QPR or anywhere else, carry on playing at grassroots level, so the week-to-week coaching is what to judge a programme on.

<ParentNote>
The main thing we got from the development centres was more football. He went to Chelsea and Palace, and the level of coaching could vary week to week depending on who was coaching that day, and so could the level of the other players. You could argue that going puts you in front of people who might have a connection, or who could put you forward to the academy coaches if you truly stand out. But as the coaches changed, I'm not sure they really had enough time to assess the players.
</ParentNote>

Take a Year 4 boy from Acton already training twice a week with his grassroots club. A Trust programme on a weeknight is a third football evening. It's worth adding if the session gives him things his club training doesn't: more touches, harder opposition, and a coach who tells him something specific about his game.

Questions to put to any programme before you commit:

- Will my child have the same coach every week, or does it rotate?
- How big are the groups, and how much of the session is spent on the ball?
- Is there any written or spoken feedback during the season?
- What happens when a session clashes with a grassroots fixture?

<ParentNote>
I'd mainly focus on whether my child enjoys it, is getting lots of good touches and is being given good feedback. I'd look at the coaches' attention to detail. Are they correcting a bad first touch or giving advice on it? Are they doing good demonstrations of skills? Can they command the attention of the group and keep them engaged? All these things point towards development. In my experience his grassroots coaches were sometimes better, as they were more invested in his development and saw him every week.
</ParentNote>

Our guide to [development centres versus academies](/academy-pathway/development-centres-vs-academies) covers the wider difference between the two.

## Safeguarding and Parent Checks

Whether your child joins a Trust programme, trials for a girls' team or is approached by the academy, these checks are worth making:

- Ask who the designated safeguarding lead is and how to raise a concern
- Check coaches hold an enhanced DBS check and FA coaching qualifications
- Ask about the policy on coaches messaging children directly, and on photos and video
- If someone at a grassroots game says they scout for QPR, confirm it with the club before passing on your child's details

The Trust publishes its safeguarding policies on the club website, and [England Football's safeguarding guidance](https://www.englandfootball.com/participate/behaviour/safeguarding) sets out what every club should have in place.

## FAQ: QPR Academy and Trials

### What category is the QPR academy?

Category Two. The club announced in January 2026 that it intends to apply for Category One, with the work expected to take around 18 months and no guarantee of success.

---

### Where does the QPR academy train?

At the TSG Training Centre in Heston, Hounslow, which it shares with the first team. It's about seven miles west of Loftus Road, beside the M4.

---

### Can my child play for a QPR Trust programme and their grassroots team?

Yes. The ADP and Showcase are run by the Trust, so your child stays registered with their grassroots club. The Under-10 girls' set-up is also built around dual registration.

---

### How do I contact QPR about youth football?

For Trust programmes, email community@qpr.co.uk. For the girls' teams and trials, email qprfcwg@qpr.co.uk. We couldn't find a published address for boys' academy trial applications; the usual route in is a scout or coach recommending a player.

## Summary

QPR's academy is Category Two, trains at Heston and is applying for Category One, a process the club expects to take about 18 months from January 2026. Boys reach it through scouting rather than open trials. The girls' teams do hold trials, usually in May with extra Under-10 dates in summer, and the Under-10 set-up lets girls stay with their grassroots team.

The Community Trust runs the paid side, from Future Hoops and Wildcats up to its ADP and the £80-a-month Showcase squad, all separate from the academy. For most families the useful question is whether the coaching is good enough to justify another evening a week, which a session or two of watching will usually answer.
