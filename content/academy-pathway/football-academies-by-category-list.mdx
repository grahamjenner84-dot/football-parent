There are 31 Category 1 academies in English football for the 2026/27 season, 17 Category 2 academies, and around 40 clubs running Category 3 or Category 4 academies through the EFL. Every club in each category is listed below, along with the changes made this summer.

Categories are set by an independent audit and can move every season, so this list is dated. It reflects the 2026/27 season, checked on 20 November 2026. For what each category means for training hours, travel and recruitment, see [academy categories explained](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained). This page is the list.

## How Many Academies Are in Each Category

- **Category 1:** 31 academies, up from 29 last season.
- **Category 2:** 17 academies, down from 20.
- **Category 3:** around 40 academies, almost all at Championship, League One and League Two clubs.
- **Category 4:** a small number. Wycombe Wanderers and York City have both said publicly that they run at Category 4.

The quickest way to see a club's category is the youth league it plays in. The Under-18 and Under-21 leagues are split by category, so the entry lists for each one double as the category lists. That is how the lists below were built, cross-checked against club announcements.

These are boys' academies under the [Elite Player Performance Plan](/academy-pathway/what-is-eppp). Girls' academies run under a separate licensing system, covered in [how girls' football academies work](/girls-football/how-girls-football-academies-work).

Category 1 status comes from the academy audit, which looks at staffing, facilities and the games programme, so it doesn't follow the first team's league. Several Championship clubs hold Category 1, a relegated club keeps its category, and Bournemouth spent three Premier League seasons with a Category 2 academy before moving up this summer.

Category 1 also changes what a club can do in recruitment. From Under-12 it can register players from anywhere in the country, while Categories 2 and 3 are limited to a 90-minute travel radius. The exact rules are set out in [academy categories explained](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained).

## What Changed for 2026/27

Five clubs changed category this summer and one opened a new academy.

- **Brentford** moved up to Category 1, four years after reopening its academy at Category 4.
- **Bournemouth** moved up to Category 1 after winning an appeal, having first had its application refused.
- **Bolton Wanderers** returned to Category 2 after six years away.
- **Watford** dropped to Category 3 after withdrawing its Under-21 side from the Professional Development League.
- **Crewe Alexandra**, a Category 2 academy since the system began in 2012, [chose to drop to Category 3](https://crewe.nub.news/news/local-sport/crewe-alex-set-to-downgrade-academy-status-in-restructure-292702), citing rising financial and operational demands.
- **York City** opened a Category 4 academy after the first team's promotion back to the EFL.

One club nearly went the other way. Wolves came close to losing Category 1 for the first time since the system began in 2012, and [kept their status after a last-minute reprieve](https://trainingground.guru/wolves-retain-category-one-status-after-last-minute-reprieve/) that depends on a planned upgrade to their Compton Park training base.

The pattern is worth knowing if your child is at a Category 2 club: the cost of running an Under-21 side is pushing some smaller clubs down a category by choice. A downgrade changes the coaching hours and the games programme, but the club still runs a licensed academy.

## How to Check a Club's Category Yourself

Because categories move, it is worth checking the club you are actually dealing with rather than relying on any list, including this one.

1. **Look at the youth league.** If the club's Under-18s play in the Under-18 Premier League, it is Category 1. The Professional Development League means Category 2. The EFL Youth Alliance means Category 3 or 4.
2. **Read the club's academy page.** Most clubs state their category, and clubs that have just moved up announce it.
3. **Ask at the first meeting.** If a club approaches your child, ask which category the academy holds and which age groups it registers. A genuine academy answers straight away.

A family in Hertfordshire with a son at Watford's academy now has a Category 3 club on the doorstep. If a Category 1 club such as Arsenal or Tottenham shows interest at Under-12 or older, it can register him regardless of distance. A Category 2 club more than 90 minutes away cannot.

A family near Bolton, on the other hand, gained a Category 2 option this summer that didn't exist a year ago. Our guide to [how to join a football academy](/academy-pathway/how-to-join-a-football-academy) covers how approaches and trials work at each level.

## What the List Doesn't Tell You

A category describes the academy's minimum standards: coaching hours, staffing, facilities and games programme. It doesn't describe the coaching culture, how much game time your child will get, or how the club treats families.

Three things parents commonly assume from a list like this, and why they don't hold:

**"A higher category is always the better move."** Category 1 squads are larger and more competitive, and a player who isn't one of the clear top performers may get fewer minutes than at a Category 2 or 3 club. Timing matters too: a player who isn't at one club's required level may be ready for another club a season later.

**"The category is permanent."** This summer alone, three clubs moved up, two moved down, and a Category 1 club came close to a downgrade. A club's category when your child joins may not be its category by the time they reach the youth development phase at Under-12.

**"A club's development centre has the same category."** Categories apply to the registered academy only. A club's development centres, soccer schools and foundation programmes sit outside the EPPP audit, as explained in [development centres vs academies](/academy-pathway/development-centres-vs-academies). Where the category does show up in family life is staffing: Category 1 academies have to employ full-time coaching staff across every phase, while at smaller academies staff often cover more than one role.

## Safeguarding and Parent Checks

Every club on this list runs a licensed academy, which is required to have a designated safeguarding lead. The list itself is also a useful check: an "academy" or "trial" advertised under a club name that doesn't appear here, or under a professional club's name but run by a third party, deserves a closer look before you pay or register anything.

Before your child joins any academy, ask:

- Who is the academy's designated safeguarding lead, and how do you contact them?
- How are coaches vetted, and is everyone working with the children DBS-checked?
- How do coaches communicate with players, and are parents copied in?
- What is the policy on photos, video and social media?
- Who do you report a concern to if it involves a coach?

The [NSPCC's Child Protection in Sport Unit](https://sport.nspcc.org.uk/) publishes guidance for parents on what good practice looks like in sport.

## FAQ: Academy Categories List

### Which Championship clubs have Category 1 academies?

It changes with promotion and relegation every season, so check the 2026/27 Championship table against the Category 1 list above. Clubs such as Birmingham City, Blackburn Rovers, Derby County and Middlesbrough held Category 1 while playing outside the Premier League.

### Does Scotland use the same categories?

No. The EPPP covers clubs in the English league system, including Welsh clubs such as Cardiff, Swansea, Newport and Wrexham. Scottish academies are graded under the Scottish FA's own system.

### How often do categories change?

Academies are audited by the Professional Game Academy Audit Company, and a club can apply to move up or choose to move down. Changes usually take effect at the start of a season, which is why this list is dated to 2026/27.
