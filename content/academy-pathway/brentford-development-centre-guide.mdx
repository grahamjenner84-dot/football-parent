## What Is the Brentford FC Development Centre?

Parents searching for a "Brentford development centre" are usually looking for one of two quite different things, and at Brentford the gap between them is bigger than at most clubs. The Football Development Centre (FDC) is run by [Brentford FC Community Sports Trust](https://www.brentfordfccst.com/en/boys-football-development-centre-fdc), the club's charitable arm, and is open to a wide range of children on a weekly basis. The [Brentford Academy](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained) is the club's formally registered talent pathway, run by the football club itself, and is far more selective.

The two aren't officially linked. A place at the Trust's Development Centre is not a step onto the academy pathway, and treating it as one sets families up for disappointment. What makes Brentford genuinely unusual, though, is the academy's own recent history: for six years the club didn't run one at all. For how Brentford compares with other clubs' academies, see our wider [list of Premier League development centres](/academy-pathway/premier-league-development-centres-list).

## Why Brentford's Academy Story Is Different

In 2016, Brentford closed its academy entirely. [Director of football Phil Giles later explained the thinking](https://sqaf.club/brentford-b-team-academy/): the club was "a very small Championship club with low revenue" and felt that if promotion to the Premier League was the target, "we had to spend every penny on that." Running a traditional academy meant developing players who could be signed away by bigger clubs for relatively small compensation fees, long before Brentford saw any benefit.

In its place, Brentford built a B team: a squad of players roughly aged 17 to 21, recruited from academy releases and late developers at other clubs rather than competing for schoolboy talent in a crowded London market. It worked. The pathway produced more than 500 first-team appearances, with players including Mads Roerslev and Yehor Yarmoliuk progressing into Premier League football for Brentford.

Following the first team's promotion to the Premier League in 2021, the club reopened its academy for the 2022/23 season, this time working within the [Elite Player Performance Plan](/academy-pathway/what-is-eppp) rather than around it. The progression since has been fast: [Category Four](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained) at reopening, Category Two within two years, and [Category One from 2026/27](https://www.brentfordfc.com/en/news/article/academy-brentford-awarded-category-one-status-premier-league-), the highest tier the Premier League awards. Academy Director David Rainford called it a status that "brings both increased opportunity and responsibility," adding that it "strengthens our ability to develop players capable of progressing to Brentford's first team." It's [reportedly the fastest progression through the EPPP system since it began in 2012](https://striver.football/the-development/brentford-category-one-academy-status).

For a parent, the practical takeaway is that Brentford's academy is young, in Premier League terms, and was rebuilt from a standing start twice in under a decade. That doesn't make it a worse environment. It does mean the club's own systems and communication style may still be maturing compared with academies that have run continuously for 20 years.

## The Trust's Football Development Centre Explained

The Boys Football Development Centre, previously known as the Advanced Training Programme, is open to boys from Year 1 through Year 11, roughly ages 5 to 16. Sessions run weekly, delivered by FA and UEFA qualified Trust coaching staff, with training venues across west London including Gunnersbury, Twickenham and Uxbridge. Players also compete in matches against other clubs' development centres over the school year, including sides from Coventry City, Fulham and Ipswich Town.

Entry is by trial, typically advertised for specific age groups a few times a year, with the club warning that places are likely to sell out quickly once dates are announced. A two-hour trial session usually splits into an hour of technical work and an hour of conditioned small-sided games, with opportunities for goalkeepers as well as outfield players.

The Trust doesn't publish a fixed national fee schedule for the FDC on its public pages, so it's worth asking directly what a place costs, what it includes, and whether siblings or existing Trust members receive any discount.

## Does Brentford Have a Development Centre for Girls?

Yes. Alongside the boys' FDC, Brentford FC Community Sports Trust runs a Girls Development Centre offering a similar weekly coaching programme for girls of primary and secondary school age. ## How the B Team Fits Alongside the Academy Today

Brentford has kept the B team running rather than retiring it now the academy is back to full strength. The club has said it intends to continue an enhanced B team games programme, including fixtures against elite European youth sides, alongside the newly Category One academy structure feeding in from Under-9s upward.

In practice, this gives Brentford two separate late-teen routes into senior football: academy graduates coming through the newly rebuilt EPPP pathway, and players signed into the B team from outside the club, often released by bigger academies in their late teens. For a family further down the age groups, this is mostly background context rather than something that affects a 7 or 10 year old directly, but it explains why Brentford's recruitment doesn't look identical to a club that has run one continuous academy system throughout.

## How to Get Your Child Seen by Brentford

There's no single guaranteed route in, but the realistic paths differ depending on which programme you mean:

- For the Trust's Football Development Centre, register interest through Brentford FC Community Sports Trust and watch for advertised trial dates, which tend to open and fill quickly for popular age groups
- For the academy itself, entry is scouting-led rather than open application. Playing regularly at a good standard of grassroots or school football is where most academy scouting happens
- Being recommended by a coach already working within the Trust's programmes can sometimes lead to further observation, though this isn't a formal pathway
- For more on how recruitment generally works across clubs, our guide to [how football clubs recruit young players](/academy-trials/how-football-clubs-recruit-young-players) is useful background

## What Brentford Looks for in Young Players

Brentford's B team model was built specifically around recruiting players other clubs released early, which says something about the club's broader philosophy: readiness and attitude at a given moment matter, but so does how a player develops over a longer timeframe. At Trust and academy level, this tends to translate into assessing players over a period of sessions rather than a single trial, with coaches watching technical grounding, response to coaching, and how a child engages with teammates alongside raw ability.

An invitation to the Football Development Centre means a Trust coach wants a longer look at your child, nothing more. Because the academy and the Trust aren't formally linked at Brentford, most FDC families are there for the coaching itself, staying at that level for as long as their child enjoys it. Genuine academy interest is far more likely to start through grassroots or school football, as covered above. Our guide on [what happens at academy trials](/academy-trials/what-happens-at-academy-trials) covers what clubs are generally watching for at this stage in more detail.

## Safeguarding and Parent Checks

Any programme involving regular sessions with your child, whether it's the Football Development Centre, the academy, or a future B team environment, is worth a few practical checks before you commit:

- Ask who the designated safeguarding lead is for the programme, and how to raise a concern
- Ask how coaches are vetted, including DBS checks and relevant FA coaching qualifications
- Check the programme's policy on communication with children outside sessions, and on photos, video and social media
- Prioritise a setting where your child feels safe and heard over any promise of a pathway to the academy

[NSPCC's Child Protection in Sport Unit](https://sport.nspcc.org.uk/) is a useful general resource for a wider checklist to work from before your child joins any club programme.

## What to Weigh Up Before Committing

Because Brentford's academy is newly Category One rather than long-established, a couple of things are worth factoring in beyond the standard development centre considerations. Recently promoted categories can mean facilities, staffing and games programmes that are still scaling up to match the new status, rather than a fully bedded-in Category One operation. That isn't a criticism, most Category One academies went through the same growth at some point, but it's a fair question to ask directly when you're weighing options against a longer-established Category One club.

It's also worth being clear-eyed that Trust programme places, academy places and any future B team recruitment are three genuinely separate things at Brentford, more so than at most clubs. Our guide to [are football development centres worth it](/parent-guides/are-football-development-centres-worth-it) covers the general trade-offs (coaching quality, travel, cost) that apply regardless of which club you're considering.

## FAQ: Brentford Development Centre

### Does Brentford FC have an academy?

Yes. Brentford closed its original academy in 2016 in favour of a B team model, then reopened the academy for the 2022/23 season. It has since progressed from Category Four to Category Two to Category One, reaching the top tier from the 2026/27 season.

---

### How do I get into the Brentford Academy?

Academy entry is scouting-led rather than open application, typically following a player being noticed at grassroots or school level, or through the Trust's Football Development Centre. There isn't a public sign-up route directly into the academy itself.

---

### How good is Brentford's academy?

Brentford holds Category One status, the highest tier under the Premier League's Elite Player Performance Plan, reached faster than any club since the system began in 2012. That reflects rapid recent investment, though it's a young Category One set-up compared with clubs that have run continuously at that level for years.

---

### What age can my child join Brentford's Football Development Centre?

The boys' FDC covers Year 1 to Year 11, roughly ages 5 to 16. The Girls Development Centre runs a similar programme for primary and secondary school age girls.

---

### Is the Football Development Centre the same as Brentford's academy?

No. The Football Development Centre is run by Brentford FC Community Sports Trust, the club's charity, and is open to a wide range of children by trial. The academy is the club's own formally registered EPPP pathway and is far more selective.

---

### Does Brentford have a development centre for girls?

Yes. The Trust runs a Girls Development Centre alongside the boys' programme.

---

## Summary

Brentford's academy has one of the more unusual recent histories in English football: closed in 2016 in favour of a B team model, reopened in 2022, and awarded Category One status just four years later. Separately, Brentford FC Community Sports Trust runs a Football Development Centre for boys and girls from around age 5 to 16, which is a genuinely different, more accessible programme from the academy itself. Treat an FDC trial invitation as what it is: the start of an observation process, not a step onto the academy pathway.
