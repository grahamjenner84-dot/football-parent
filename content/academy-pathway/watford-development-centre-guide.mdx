## What Is the Watford FC Development Centre?

Most trials and development centres advertised under the Watford name are run by Watford FC's Community Sports and Education Trust (the CSE Trust), the club's charity, rather than by the academy itself. The Trust runs the [Player Development Programme](https://www.watfordfccsetrust.com/project/player-development-programmes/), which most families mean when they talk about "the Watford development centre".

The Watford Academy is the club's registered talent pathway under the [Elite Player Performance Plan](/academy-pathway/what-is-eppp), trains at the club's training ground on Bell Lane, London Colney, and recruits mainly through scouting.

The two sit side by side, but they are different programmes with different entry routes. Most of the confusion parents run into at Watford comes from treating a development centre place as an academy place, or from assuming the academy holds open trials that anyone can book. For how this split works across clubs generally, see our guide to [development centres vs academies](/academy-pathway/development-centres-vs-academies).

## The Three Tiers of Watford's Player Development Programme

The Trust describes its programme as three tiers, each with a different way in.

**Tier 1** is the entry level. The Trust says there are no entry requirements and no trial: bookings are open to any grassroots player who wants an extra skills session each week. It's a weekly coaching session open to anyone, though the coaches are still watching: a Tier 1 player who stands out can be put forward for a Tier 2 trial.

**Tier 2, the Player Development Centre (PDC)**, is for what the Trust calls "technically competent grassroots football players". Places are given out by trial. PDC players get one session a week plus fixtures. Boys' sessions run on Monday evenings between 6pm and 9pm, split into age bands from Under-7 to Under-16. Girls' PDC groups for the same age range run at centres in St Albans and Kings Langley.

**Tier 3, the Advanced PDC**, is invite only. It's aimed at players "performing at a consistently high level of technical competency" for their grassroots team, and it doubles the commitment to two sessions a week plus extra fixtures:

- Monday: Under-7 to Under-12 for one hour, Under-13 to Under-16 for an hour and a half
- Friday: Under-7 to Under-10 for one hour, Under-11 to Under-16 for an hour and a half

The 2026/27 programme runs from early September 2026 to early July 2027, with payment plans available for both the PDC and the Advanced PDC.

### The futsal option

Watford's programme also has a futsal strand: an open Futsal PDC of 28 sessions from September to April, and an Advanced Futsal PDC on Wednesday evenings. For a child who already plays 9-a-side or 11-a-side on Sundays, futsal's smaller court and low-bounce ball mean a lot more touches under pressure, a different kind of session from a second night of grass-pitch drills.

## How Trials and Invitations Work

Moving between the tiers is driven by the coaches. In the Trust's words, Tier 1 players who excel "can be recommended for trial" for Tier 2. Advanced PDC places come the same way: a PDC coach sends a trial recommendation for a player who is consistently performing above the group.

The timing catches some families out. Trials for the 2026/27 boys' and girls' PDC had already finished by the start of the season, and the Trust's page says places have been allocated, with a waitlist form for future trials to follow. If your child is starting to look for a development centre in October, the realistic options at Watford are a Tier 1 booking now, or the waitlist for the next round of PDC trials.

One detail in the Trust's wording is worth noticing. When coaches think a player is ready for an academy trial, the Trust says it will "send a trial recommendation to local professional football academies". It says academies, plural, and doesn't name only Watford's.

Within reach of Hertfordshire there are several professional academies, including Arsenal's Hale End and Tottenham's set-up. A strong Watford PDC player may therefore be put forward to a different club's academy. The Trust is the club's community charity, and its coaches are recommending players to whichever local academy suits them.

If you want to compare, our [Arsenal development centre guide](/academy-pathway/arsenal-development-centre-guide) covers how the club down the road runs its programmes.

## Does Watford Have a Development Centre for Girls?

Yes, at two levels. Girls can join the Trust's PDC groups at St Albans and Kings Langley, as above. Separately, the Trust runs one of the FA's [Girls' Emerging Talent Centres](https://www.englandfootball.com/england/youth/womens-talent-pathway/Emerging-Talent-Centres), part of the national network that, together with Professional Game Academies, took over from the old [Regional Talent Clubs](/girls-football/girls-rtcs-explained).

Watford's ETC is for girls from Under-9 to Under-13, runs on Friday evenings from 5.30pm to 7pm for 30 weeks of the year, and is free. Sessions take place at the Watford FC Training Ground on Bell Lane, so girls in this programme train at the club's own base, which isn't true of every club's development centre. Fixtures are played against grassroots clubs, player development groups and other ETCs.

Because ETCs are part of the FA's national talent pathway rather than a paid club programme, they work differently from the boys' PDC. Our guide to [Emerging Talent Centres](/girls-football/emerging-talent-centres-explained) explains how girls are identified and what sits above an ETC.

## Watford's Academy and the Move to Category Three

In May 2026, Watford withdrew its Under-21 side from the Professional Development League. As a result, [the academy drops to Category Three from the 2026/27 season](https://trainingground.guru/watford-academy-downgraded-after-pulling-u21s-out-of-league/). The club said it had carried out a "lengthy and strategic" review and wants a closer link between its Under-18 side and the first team, which it believes will "improve overall standards and opportunities for our best Academy players".

Parents of younger children reading "downgraded" sometimes assume the academy is closing its lower age groups. Nothing in the club's announcement says that. The change sits at the top of the pathway, among the 17 to 21 year olds, where the club wants its best Under-18s closer to the first team instead of playing an Under-21 league programme.

Category Three academies still run a full pathway from the Under-9s upward. The difference from Categories One and Two is mainly resources.

The [Premier League's guide for academy parents](https://www.premierleague.com/en/footballandcommunity/youth-development/parent-hub/the-academy-experience) sets Category Three coaching at 4 hours a week in the foundation phase (Under-9 to Under-11) and 5 rising to 7 hours from Under-12 to Under-16. A Category One academy offers up to 8 hours a week for its older foundation-phase players and up to 12 hours from Under-12 to Under-16. Our guide to [academy categories](/academy-pathway/academy-categories-explained) sets out what each tier requires.

## Routes Into the Watford Academy

There isn't a public sign-up form for the academy itself. Realistically, players reach it in one of these ways:

- **Being scouted at grassroots or school football.** A lot of academy recruitment still starts with someone watching a Sunday or school match. Our guide to [how football clubs recruit young players](/academy-trials/how-football-clubs-recruit-young-players) explains how that usually works.
- **A recommendation from the Trust's PDC coaches.** As above, that recommendation can go to any local academy, not only Watford.
- **Being invited to an academy trial period** after a scout or coach has watched a player more than once. That's often several sessions with an age group rather than a single day. Our guide to [what happens at academy trials](/academy-trials/what-happens-at-academy-trials) covers what coaches watch for.

Be cautious about anything sold as a "Watford academy trial" that you pay to attend. Paid camps and coaching sessions in the Watford area, including the Trust's own holiday camps, can be good football, but booking one is not the same as being invited to trial by the academy.

## What a Season at the Advanced PDC Looks Like for a Family

To see what the commitment means in practice, take an Under-11 who plays for a grassroots team and is invited into the Advanced PDC. Their week could be:

- Monday: Advanced PDC, 90 minutes
- Wednesday: grassroots team training
- Friday: Advanced PDC, 90 minutes
- Weekend: grassroots league match, plus occasional PDC fixtures in the school holidays

That's four football days a week for a ten year old, with a Monday and Friday evening drive for a parent. It's manageable for many families, but it's worth agreeing before the season starts what happens if your child is tired, or if a grassroots fixture clashes with a PDC game.

A few questions are worth asking the Trust before you sign up or accept an invitation:

- How long will my child stay with the same coach, and how is progress fed back to us?
- How many players are in each age group, and how much match time do they get in fixtures?
- What does the payment plan cover, and is any of it refundable if my child stops partway through?
- If my child is recommended to an academy, how is that communicated, and to which clubs?

Our guide to [whether football development centres are worth it](/parent-guides/are-football-development-centres-worth-it) has a fuller set of trade-offs to weigh up, including cost, travel and burnout.

## Safeguarding and Parent Checks

Whether your child joins a Tier 1 session, the Advanced PDC, the girls' ETC or, later, an academy trial, a few checks are worth making:

- Find out who the designated safeguarding lead is for the programme, and how to raise a concern with the Trust or the club
- Ask how coaches are vetted, including enhanced DBS checks and FA coaching qualifications
- Check the policy on coaches contacting children directly, and on photos, video and social media from sessions and fixtures
- If a third party approaches you claiming links to Watford's academy, check with the club directly before sharing your child's details

The [NSPCC's guidance on keeping children safe in sport](https://www.nspcc.org.uk/keeping-children-safe/outside-the-home/sports-clubs/) is a useful checklist before your child starts with any club programme.

## FAQ: Watford Academy and Development Centre

### Does Watford hold open academy trials?

Not in the sense of a public trial day anyone can book. Academy places come through scouting and invitation. The open trials parents usually find are for the Trust's Player Development Centre, which is a separate programme.

---

### What age can my child join the Watford development centre?

The Trust's Player Development Programme runs from Under-7 to Under-16 for both boys and girls. The girls' Emerging Talent Centre covers Under-9 to Under-13.

---

### How much does the Watford Player Development Centre cost?

The Trust doesn't publish a single headline fee on its programme page. Prices sit on its booking system, and payment plans are available for both the PDC and the Advanced PDC. The girls' ETC is free.

---

### Is Watford's academy still worth joining after dropping to Category Three?

That depends on the child. The change mainly affects the oldest age groups. A Category Three academy usually means fewer coaching hours and smaller staff teams than higher categories, and it can also mean smaller squads and more individual attention. For a younger player, the coaching in their own age group is something you can see and judge directly, so weigh it alongside the category.

---

### Where does Watford's academy train?

At the Watford FC Training Ground on Bell Lane, London Colney, near St Albans, which the club shares with its first team.

## Summary

Watford's development centres are run by the club's Community Sports and Education Trust in three tiers: an open Tier 1, a trial-based PDC and an invite-only Advanced PDC, plus futsal programmes and a free Emerging Talent Centre for girls. Coaches decide who moves up the tiers, and the Trust says strong players can be recommended to local professional academies, not only Watford's.

The academy itself recruits by scouting and invitation, and moves to Category Three from 2026/27 after the club stepped back from Under-21 football. That change sits at the top of the pathway, among the oldest players. For families, the most useful thing to know is which of these programmes you're actually looking at, because each one is entered a different way.
