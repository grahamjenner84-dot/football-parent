A football rebounder sends the ball back every time your child passes or shoots at it, so they can practise first touch, passing and volleys on their own, without a parent fetching the ball or standing in goal. There are two main types. A **rebound board** is a low, solid panel that returns ground passes quickly and predictably. A **net rebounder** is a framed net, usually angled, that returns the ball higher and slower, which suits volleys, chest control and younger children who need more time on the ball.

Which one is right depends on what your child wants to practise, how old they are, and whether it is going on a lawn, 3G or a patio.

## Rebound Boards vs Net Rebounders

The two types look similar in a product listing and behave very differently in a garden.

**Rebound boards** are a rigid panel, usually polypropylene, on weighted legs. They sit low to the ground and return a pass along the floor at close to the speed it arrived. That makes them a passing wall: the same thing a child gets from kicking a ball against the side of the house, without the noise or the windows. The QuickPlay REPLAY boards have two faces, a flat one for a predictable return and a ribbed one that sends the ball back at slightly random angles, which makes the first touch harder to plan for.

**Net rebounders** are a steel or fibreglass frame with a tensioned net, set at an angle. The net absorbs some of the pace and throws the ball back up and out, so the return comes back higher and slower. That is what makes them good for volleys, controlling a dropping ball on the thigh or chest, and heading practice for older players. Many have an adjustable frame, so the same net can be set more upright for ground passes or tilted back for balls in the air.

A rough rule:

- **Mostly passing and first touch on the ground:** a rebound board.
- **Volleys, aerial control and a slower return for a young child:** a net rebounder.
- **Not enough room for a goal and a rebounder:** a combined goal and rebounder frame.

A rebound board is also much smaller to store. The REPLAY Station folds flat, which matters if it has to come into the shed or the hallway after every session.

## Which Rebounder for Which Age

**Under 7 and Under 8.** A net rebounder with a slow return, or a standard rebound board set for ground passes. At this age the aim is lots of touches, and a ball that comes back too fast is just a ball to chase. Use the same size ball they play with, which is a size 3 until Under 11 (see [football sizes by age](/football-gear/best-footballs-by-age)).

**Under 9 to Under 11.** This is when a rebound board starts to earn its place. Children are passing with more pace and starting to use both feet, and a board gives them a passing partner who never gets bored. A board with a lifted setting, like the REPLAY Station, adds half-volleys once ground passing is going well.

**Under 12 and up.** Older children strike the ball harder and want more variety: volleys, chest control, one-touch passing at pace. A wider board such as the REPLAY Station XL, or a heavier adjustable net, copes better with a ball hit at full power. A light frame that tips over when a size 4 or 5 is driven into it gets frustrating quickly.

Whatever the age, a rebounder is a practice tool for a child who already wants to practise. It will not create the motivation on its own, and if your child is getting a lot of football already, our guide on [how much training is too much](/football-development/how-much-training-is-too-much) is worth a read before adding daily sessions at home.

## Surface, Space and Weather

**Grass.** Ground stakes hold a net rebounder well on a lawn. Boards rely on their weight and sandbag. Both will wear a bare patch in front of them if they stay in one spot, so move them around now and then.

**3G and artificial lawns.** Stakes are no use here. Look for a rebounder with weighted feet or one that takes base weights or a sandbag. Some artificial lawns at home have a thin pile over hard ground, which also makes the ball run faster than on a club 3G pitch.

**Patio and hard standing.** A rebound board or a frame with rubber feet works best. Bear in mind that a ball returning off a hard surface skids and bounces more than off grass, which is harder for younger children to control.

**Space.** Allow room behind the player as well as in front of the rebounder. A child needs to stand several metres back to make a proper pass, and the ball that gets past them needs somewhere to go that is not a neighbour's garden or a greenhouse.

**Weather.** Every QuickPlay rebounder we have covered here carries guidance not to leave it outdoors for long periods, and on some models leaving it out voids the warranty. Dry it before putting it away after rain. A board that folds flat is far easier to bring in than a net frame that has to be taken apart.

## How to Use a Rebounder at Home

A rebounder gets the most use when your child has a few simple games to play against it rather than kicking it aimlessly. Some that work well:

- **Two-touch passing.** Pass, control with one touch, pass back with the second. Count how many in a row before a mistake.
- **Weaker foot only.** The same game, weaker foot only. Children avoid their weaker foot in matches, and a rebounder is a low-pressure place to use it.
- **Inside, outside, sole.** Control the return with a different part of the foot each time, to build the first-touch range coaches look for.
- **Half-volleys and volleys.** On a lifted setting or a tilted net, strike the ball before it bounces, or on the half-volley, keeping it low.
- **Turn and finish.** If there is a goal in the garden, control the return, turn and shoot. This is where a combined goal and rebounder frame earns its money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a football rebounder?

A football rebounder is a board or net that returns the ball to the player when they pass or shoot at it. It lets a child practise passing, first touch and volleys on their own, without someone to pass back or fetch the ball.

### Is a rebound board or a net rebounder better?

A rebound board is better for ground passing and first touch, because it returns the ball quickly and predictably along the floor. A net rebounder is better for volleys, aerial control and younger children, because the return is slower and higher.

### What age is a football rebounder suitable for?

Most rebounders are listed for children aged 4 or 5 and up. Younger children usually get on better with a slow-returning net or a board set for ground passes, while older children need a wider board or a heavier frame that copes with harder shots.

### Can you use a football rebounder on a patio or 3G?

Yes, but check how it is anchored. Ground stakes only work on grass. On a patio, 3G or an artificial lawn, choose a board with a weighted base or sandbag, or a net frame with rubber feet or base weights.

### Can a football rebounder be left outside?

Most manufacturers advise against leaving a rebounder outdoors for long periods, and for some models it voids the warranty. A rebound board that folds flat is the easiest type to bring in after each session.

### Does a football rebounder actually help a child improve?

It gives a child far more touches of the ball than they would get waiting for someone to pass to them, especially on their weaker foot. The improvement comes from regular, focused use, such as short passing and control games, rather than from owning one.
