Search "what qualifications do I need to be a football coach" and most of what comes back is written for someone chasing a coaching career, not a parent who has just agreed to take their child's grassroots team on a Tuesday evening. The honest answer for that second group is shorter than the course pages suggest, and worth setting out plainly before getting into the detail of what each qualification actually involves.

If you've already decided to volunteer and want the full picture, including DBS checks, time commitment and coaching your own child, see the [full guide to becoming a grassroots football coach](/parent-guides/how-to-become-a-football-coach). This one is specifically about the qualifications question: what's required, what each course actually covers, what it costs, and whether it's worth doing.

## Do You Need Qualifications to Coach Grassroots Football?

No, not to start. [EE Playmaker by England Football](https://learn.englandfootball.com/courses/football/ee-playmaker) is free, online, takes about five hours across twelve modules, and assumes no previous football experience or qualifications. It's built specifically for people helping out at a club rather than pursuing a coaching career, and it's enough to begin as an assistant coach at most grassroots clubs.

Where a formal qualification becomes necessary is if you take on the named head coach role rather than assisting. At that point your club or league will usually expect you to be working towards, or to already hold, [Introduction to Coaching Football](https://learn.englandfootball.com/courses/football/introduction-to-coaching-football), alongside a Safeguarding Children course, a first-aid qualification and an in-date DBS check. None of that requires prior coaching experience or a playing background. Many clubs will let a new head coach start the season while these are booked in, provided they're completed within an agreed window.

A common pattern at grassroots level is assisting for a full season on Playmaker alone, then deciding whether to take on a team as head coach the following year once you know whether the time commitment actually works for your family. There's no expectation that you go from parent volunteer to fully qualified head coach in one summer, and plenty of clubs would rather have a committed assistant than a head coach who's rushed through courses to get there.

## What Is Introduction to Coaching Football (Formerly FA Level 1)?

Introduction to Coaching Football replaced [the old FA Level 1 in Coaching Football](https://www.berks-bucksfa.com/coaches/england-football-courses/introduction-to-coaching-football). The FA Level 1 name still shows up in search results and on some County FA pages, because plenty of coaches and clubs still refer to it that way, but Introduction to Coaching Football is the current course.

It's delivered digitally: a mix of self-paced online material and live webinars with a coach developer, rather than the weekend-long in-person format the old Level 1 was known for. That shift matters for grassroots parents specifically, since it's far easier to fit around a full-time job and a family than a fixed Saturday course used to be. It covers session design, practical coaching skills and how to plan and deliver a training session for youth players, going a step beyond what Playmaker covers.

## What Is the UEFA C Diploma (Formerly FA Level 2)?

The UEFA C Diploma has replaced [the old FA Level 2 in Coaching Football](https://learn.englandfootball.com/courses/football/uefa-c-licence). It's a bigger commitment than either of the courses above: six face-to-face course days, building on the coaching skills and session-planning knowledge from Introduction to Coaching Football rather than starting from scratch.

It isn't open to everyone straight away, either. [To enrol](https://help.thefa.com/support/solutions/articles/7000068778-what-are-the-pre-requisites-for-attending-the-uefa-c-) you need to have already completed Introduction to Coaching Football (or the old FA Level 1), hold valid Introduction to First Aid in Football and Safeguarding Children certificates, and be actively coaching a team in a season-long competition in England. In practice, that means UEFA C is a course you grow into after a season or two coaching, not something you book alongside Playmaker in your first few weeks.

This is where the gap between "coaching my son's Sunday team" and "working towards a coaching pathway" starts to show. Six full days is a genuine ask against a normal working week, and it's aimed at coaches who want a deeper, more structured grounding in tactical and technical development, not just at people trying to run a fair, well-organised session.

## Is It Worth Doing the UEFA C Diploma as a Grassroots Coach?

For most parents coaching a grassroots youth team, no, not immediately, and that's a genuinely defensible position rather than a compromise. Plenty of grassroots coaches run a full season, or several, on Introduction to Coaching Football alone, and the day-to-day reality of grassroots coaching is organisational as much as tactical: turning up prepared, keeping a session moving, communicating with parents, and making sure every child gets game time. A [good football development environment](/football-development/good-football-development-environment) at this level tends to come from consistency and care rather than technical sophistication.

That said, it's worth doing if any of the following apply: you're coaching an older age group where tactical and technical development becomes a bigger part of sessions, you're likely to stay involved with the club beyond your own child's time there, or you're genuinely interested in coaching as more than a short-term volunteer role. None of those are requirements, just reasons some parent coaches choose to go further.

<ParentNote>
The main thing Level 2 gave me at the time was showing me how to actually run a session: having a clear structure for the warm-up and one session topic, rather than trying to cover multiple things in one go. It also covered things like how to correct a player when needed and how to demonstrate properly. I planned my sessions carefully at the time because I needed to for the course, which meant I built up a session library, but reflecting on what worked and what didn't probably did more for my development as a coach than the qualification itself. Coaching hours matter just as much as any course: the more you practise, the more feedback you get and the more you develop.
</ParentNote>

One of the underrated parts of doing the course was meeting other coaches in similar positions, with similar challenges and worries, and getting to see what they did well and not so well. That kind of exposure doesn't stop once the course ends, either.

Having a son who's been to different sessions at different clubs has probably helped my own coaching just as much as the course did. I watch other coaches and pick up new drills, or better ways of delivering ones I already use. ## How Much Do Football Coaching Qualifications Cost?

- **EE Playmaker**: free.
- **Introduction to Coaching Football**: £100.
- **UEFA C Diploma**: £650, reduced to £500 if you coach at a Grassroots Affiliated Club, where £150 is funded.

Course fees aren't necessarily coming out of your own pocket. Many grassroots clubs will pay for or contribute towards a parent's course when they volunteer to coach, particularly for the entry-level and Introduction courses, since the club benefits directly from having another qualified volunteer. It's worth asking your club's welfare officer or committee what support is available before you book and pay for anything yourself.

## How Do You Book Football Coaching Courses?

Courses are booked through [England Football Learning](https://learn.englandfootball.com/courses/football), which lists current dates, locations and online options. You'll need an FA Number before enrolling, which your club can usually help you set up if you don't already have one. Your County FA can also advise on which course fits your role, whether that's assisting occasionally, becoming a named assistant, or taking on a team as head coach.

Managing the practical side of actually running a fair, well-organised team, rotations, minutes and keeping track of who's played where, is a separate problem from the qualifications question covered here. Our guide to [equal playing time in grassroots football](/coaching/equal-playing-time-in-grassroots-football) covers how to actually calculate and track it, and it's one a growing number of grassroots coaches are now solving with dedicated team management tools rather than a notebook and a spreadsheet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to be qualified to coach my kids' football team?

Not to start as an assistant. EE Playmaker is free and requires no prior experience or qualifications. A named head coach role usually requires Introduction to Coaching Football, safeguarding training, a first-aid course and a DBS check, none of which require a coaching or playing background.

### What is FA Level 1 called now?

Introduction to Coaching Football. It replaced the FA Level 1 in Coaching Football, and is delivered as a mix of online modules and live webinars rather than the previous in-person format.

### What is FA Level 2 called now?

The UEFA C Diploma. It replaced the FA Level 2 in Coaching Football and involves six face-to-face course days.

### Do I need to have played football to become a coach?

No. None of England Football's entry-level or Introduction courses require previous playing experience, and most grassroots clubs are short of volunteers rather than turning down parents without a playing background.
