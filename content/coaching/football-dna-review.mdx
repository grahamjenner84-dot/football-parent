Football DNA gets 5 out of 5 from us. At £7.99 a month it is worth it for any grassroots coach who plans training around a theme, and especially for newer coaches. It is less useful for a coach who wants a quick drill on the way to the pitch. The library of filmed sessions is the reason to pay. You read the plan and the coaching points, then watch an experienced coach deliver the same session, correct players and move it on.

## What Football DNA Is

Football DNA is a coaching content platform. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/football-dna/id1611898337) describes more than 1,000 sessions on video. Coaching points appear on screen and each session comes as a downloadable PDF. It also lists sports science and conditioning content, interviews, webinars and a session builder called My DNA.

For a grassroots coach, the core of it is simple. You search for what you want to work on, such as playing out from the back, 1v1s or defending as a pair. You filter by age group, then pick a full session.

A drill library gives you an activity. A session gives you a warm-up, a main practice, progressions and a game, all built around the same theme. Football DNA is built around sessions.

It is a well-reviewed product. It holds a [5.0 TrustScore on Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/footballdna.co.uk), with reviewers repeatedly mentioning time saved on planning. Trustpilot notes that the company has not recently invited customers to leave reviews.

## Using It for a Real Problem: Playing Out From the Back at U8

The best test of a coaching app is a problem from your own Saturday games, not a demo.

<ParentNote>
For me it's great when I have something I want to work on. For example, at U8 we would sometimes struggle working the ball out of defence. I had ideas on what we could do, but I went to Football DNA and saw a whole session based on it.
</ParentNote>

Playing out from the back at U8 is a common sticking point. At mini-soccer goal kicks, the opposition has to drop back to the halfway line, which is the retreat line set out in [the FA's youth officiating guidance](https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/hampshirefa/2020/referee/guidance-for-officials---officiating-youth-football---november-2020.ashx). That gives young teams time on the ball, but many still hurry it forward or lose it in front of their own goal.

A coach in that position often has half an idea: get the defenders wider, get someone to show for the ball. What they are missing is a practice that builds it step by step.

That is where a themed session helps. It starts with an unopposed or lightly opposed pattern and adds pressure in stages. It finishes in a game where the habit has to show up. The coaching points tell you the two or three things to look for, so you aren't trying to fix everything at once.

The age filter matters too. A session designed for older players can ask U8s to read too much, and the filter keeps you to practices pitched at the right level.

## The Filmed Sessions Are the Real Value

Plenty of free websites will give you a diagram of a practice. Very few show you how that practice is coached.

<ParentNote>
As well as being able to read the session plan and coaching points, I could also watch the session coached by top coaches like Paul Barry. So aside from the one session, you are also getting to watch how top coaches communicate and how they explain it. You see what bits they pick up and correct, and how they let the session flow. You also get to see how they advance and develop the themes.
</ParentNote>

For a volunteer coach this is the part that is hard to get anywhere else. Most grassroots coaches never watch another coach run a session. They coach alone with the players in front of them.

Watching an experienced coach shows you things a written plan can't:

- **When to stop the session and when to let it run.** A plan can't tell you how often to stop play. Watching shows you how little some coaches interrupt.
- **How short the explanations are.** Young players switch off quickly, and the videos show how much can be said in a few seconds.
- **What gets corrected first.** In a playing-out practice, the first thing picked up is often body shape or the angle to receive, not the pass itself.
- **When to progress.** Moving to the next stage when most players have got it, rather than when all of them have, keeps a session flowing.

Paul Barry, who coaches in many of the videos, set out his views on early-years development in our [FutureFit interview with Football DNA](/parent-guides/futurefit-football-dna-interview-part-1). It's useful background on the thinking behind the content.

## Is £7.99 a Month Worth It?

At £7.99 a month, Football DNA costs about £96 a year. For a coach running one or two sessions a week through a season, that is a few pence per session planned.

Whether it is worth it depends on what you are paying for. The session diagrams alone would be hard to justify, because free diagrams are easy to find. The filmed delivery, the progressions and the time saved planning are what you are buying.

A useful way to judge it: if you would spend 30 minutes or more searching for and adapting a session each week, the subscription is probably paying for itself in time.

If you coach a single team and only need ideas occasionally, it is worth subscribing for a month around a specific block of work. Cancel once you have what you need, then come back.

Check the current price and any annual option on [Football DNA's website](https://footballdna.co.uk) before subscribing, as both can change.

## What Could Be Better

- **No real free tier.** You can't properly judge the library before paying, which matters for a volunteer coach paying out of their own pocket.
- **The cost is per coach.** A club with several age groups will want to ask Football DNA about club access rather than each coach paying separately.
- **It needs a bit of planning time.** Watching a full session takes longer than glancing at a diagram, so it works best when you plan a day or two ahead rather than in the car park.
- **Sessions still need adapting.** A filmed session assumes a certain number of players and space. On a night when eight players turn up instead of twelve, you will need to shrink the area or change the numbers yourself.

## Who Should Pay for Football DNA, and Who Shouldn't

**Pay for it if:**

- You coach a grassroots team and want to work on themes, not random drills
- You are a newer coach and want to see how a session is delivered, not just set up
- You keep finding the same few drills in your sessions and want fresh ideas

**Don't pay for it if:**

- You want an app that handles availability, payments or playing time. That is a different job: see our roundup of the [best grassroots football apps](/coaching/best-grassroots-football-apps)
- You only coach occasionally and are happy with free diagrams, such as the practices in our guide to [football drills for 7 and 8 year olds](/coaching/football-drills-for-7-and-8-year-olds)
- Your club already provides session plans through its own coaching lead or the County FA

## Football DNA Alternatives

- **England Football Learning.** The FA's [England Football Learning](https://learn.englandfootball.com/) site publishes its own session programmes and coaching articles. It's worth looking at first, because it is the governing body's own material.
- **Your club's coaching lead.** Many clubs share session plans between age groups. It costs nothing to ask before subscribing to anything.
- **Free drill sites and our own guides.** Fine for single activities. They rarely give you a full themed session or show it being coached.

Football DNA is for planning training. For the match-day side, such as rotating players fairly and tracking minutes, [Football Parent's Coach App](/football-parent-coach-app) covers that job and can sit alongside it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Football DNA have a free trial?

There is no meaningful free tier for the main library at the time of writing. Check Football DNA's website for any current offer before subscribing.

### Is Football DNA on iPhone and Android?

The app is listed on the App Store for iPhone and iPad. We could not find an Android listing at the time of writing, so check Football DNA's website for current access options if you use an Android phone.

### Is Football DNA suitable for beginner coaches?

Yes. Beginner coaches arguably get the most from it, because the filmed sessions show how a practice is delivered as well as how it is set up. Several Trustpilot reviewers say the same.

### Can a whole club use one Football DNA account?

Football DNA offers club access, which is usually a better fit than coaches sharing one login. Ask them directly about pricing for the number of coaches you have.
