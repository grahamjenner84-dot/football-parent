Football DNA is worth it for any grassroots coach who plans training around a theme, especially newer coaches, and it gets 5 out of 5 from us. It costs £7.99 a month or £59.99 a year, with no free trial. The library of filmed sessions is the reason to pay. You read the plan and the coaching points, then watch an experienced coach deliver the same session, correct players and move it on.

This review is based on more than a year of paid membership, bought with our own money.

## Using It for a Real Problem: Playing Out From the Back at U8

The best test of a coaching app is a problem from your own Saturday games, not a demo.

<ParentNote>
For me it's great when I have something I want to work on. For example, at U8 we would sometimes struggle working the ball out of defence. I had ideas on what we could do, but I went to Football DNA and saw a whole session based on it.

In my head, I wanted to give the goalkeeper more options, but I wanted an easy structure that the kids could understand. Secondary to that was how I would then explain it to them and get them to practise it.
</ParentNote>

Playing out from the back at U8 is a common sticking point. At mini-soccer goal kicks, the opposition has to drop back to the halfway line, which is the retreat line set out in [the FA's youth officiating guidance](https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/hampshirefa/2020/referee/guidance-for-officials---officiating-youth-football---november-2020.ashx). That gives young teams time on the ball, but many still hurry it forward or lose it in front of their own goal.

A coach in that position often has half an idea: get the defenders wider, get someone to show for the ball. What they are missing is a practice that builds it step by step.

That is where a themed session helps. It starts with an unopposed or lightly opposed pattern and adds pressure in stages. It finishes in a game where the habit has to show up. The coaching points tell you the two or three things to look for, so you aren't trying to fix everything at once.

The age filter matters too. A session designed for older players can ask U8s to read too much, and the filter keeps you to practices pitched at the right level.

Filtering by U5 to U8 and the topic "Playing Out From The Back" brings up a run of sessions on exactly that problem. The one Graham ran with his U8 team is the playing out from the back 5v5 game, shown in the video above and the screenshots below. Its details give the duration (20 minutes), the numbers it works with (6 to 10 players), the age ranges and the equipment.

The progressions are concrete. One is a touch limit on the team in possession. Another is a time limit on the build-up, where a goal scored within it counts double. There is also a regression for when the practice is too hard, which is often the case at U8.

## What Is Football DNA?

Football DNA is a coaching platform with a library of filmed sessions and practices. Each one comes with a session plan, coaching points, a diagram and a downloadable PDF. There are also week-by-week curriculums by age group, and a session builder for putting your own sessions together. You can use it on the website or in the app on iPhone, iPad and Android.

You search for what you want to work on, filter by age group and pick a full session: a warm-up, a main practice, progressions and a game, all built around the same theme.

## The Filmed Sessions Are the Real Value

Plenty of free websites will give you a diagram of a practice. Very few show you how that practice is coached.

<ParentNote>
As well as being able to read the session plan and coaching points, I could also watch the session coached by top coaches like Paul Barry. So aside from the one session, you are also getting to watch how top coaches communicate and how they explain it. You see what bits they pick up and correct, and how they let the session flow. You also get to see how they advance and develop the themes.
</ParentNote>

For a volunteer coach this is the part that is hard to get anywhere else. Most grassroots coaches never watch another coach run a session. They coach alone with the players in front of them.

Watching an experienced coach shows you things a written plan can't:

- **When to stop the session and when to let it run.** A plan can't tell you how often to stop play. Watching shows you how little some coaches interrupt.
- **How short the explanations are.** Young players switch off quickly, and the videos show how much can be said in a few seconds.
- **What gets corrected first.** In a playing-out practice, the first thing picked up is often body shape or the angle to receive, not the pass itself.
- **When to progress.** Moving to the next stage when most players have got it, rather than when all of them have, keeps a session flowing.

Paul Barry, who coaches in many of the videos, set out his views on early-years development in our [FutureFit interview with Football DNA](/parent-guides/futurefit-football-dna-interview-part-1). It's useful background on the thinking behind the content.

## The Football DNA Curriculum: A Week-by-Week Plan

Searching for a session works when you already know the problem. The curriculums are for the weeks when you don't. You pick your team's age range and follow a plan that runs week by week.

The U5 to U8 curriculum is set out in blocks of weeks, and each week has its own theme. Block One runs from hiding and carrying the ball, through passing and receiving, ball striking and 1v1s, to a games week in week six. Block Two then comes back round to the same skills, starting with moving and ball manipulation.

That shape suits young players. They get a few weeks on each core skill and then return to it later, rather than being expected to pick a skill up from one session and remember it all season.

Each week is a full 60-minute session for 6 to 10 players, with a session objective and three practices. Block One, Week Two (hiding and carrying the ball) runs:

- **Traffic Lights:** a dribbling warm-up where the coach holds up a coloured cone for go, ball mastery or stop
- **Shadow Tag:** pairs, each with a ball, trying to stop their ball in their partner's shadow
- **A 2v2 "Half Way There" small-sided game:** players have to dribble into the other half to score

Each practice has its own coaching points, a progression to make it harder and a regression to make it easier. The full session PDF prints the whole plan on one page, with a QR code that links to the video.

## How Much Does Football DNA Cost?

Football DNA costs £7.99 a month or £59.99 a year, which works out at about £5 a month on the annual plan, and there is no free trial.

Football DNA says prices rise on 1 January, and existing members keep their price while they stay members. Prices include VAT where it applies.

Paying monthly costs about £96 over a year. For a coach running one or two sessions a week through a season, either plan works out at well under £1 per session planned.

Whether it is worth it depends on what you are paying for. The session diagrams alone would be hard to justify, because free diagrams are easy to find. The filmed delivery, the progressions, the curriculums and the time saved planning are what you are buying.

A useful way to judge it: if you would spend 30 minutes or more searching for and adapting a session each week, the subscription is probably paying for itself in time.

**Which plan to pick:**

- **Try the free section first.** It includes full filmed sessions, so you can see how the coaching videos work before paying anything.
- **Monthly** suits a coach who wants it for one block of work, or who isn't sure they will use it every week. There is no money-back guarantee, but you can cancel before the next month.
- **Annual** is cheaper if you coach all season. It is the only plan with the 14-day money-back guarantee.

At checkout you are asked to give up the usual 14-day right to cancel in exchange for immediate access, so on the monthly plan the first month is paid either way.

## What Could Be Better

- **No free trial.** The free section gives a taste, but you can't try the full library before paying. Only the annual plan has a money-back guarantee.
- **The cost is per coach.** Club membership starts at five coaches and is priced on request, so a smaller group of coaches pays individually.
- **Some sessions still need adapting on the night.** Each session lists the player numbers it works with and a regression, which helps. On a night when attendance falls outside that range, you will still need to change the area or the teams yourself.

## Who Should Pay for Football DNA, and Who Shouldn't

**Pay for it if:**

- You coach a grassroots team and want to work on themes, not random drills
- You are a newer coach and want to see how a session is delivered, not just set up
- You keep finding the same few drills in your sessions and want fresh ideas

**Don't pay for it if:**

- You want an app that handles availability, payments or playing time. That is a different job: see our roundup of the [best grassroots football apps](/coaching/best-grassroots-football-apps)
- You only coach occasionally, and Football DNA's free section covers what you need
- Your club already provides session plans through its own coaching lead or the County FA

## Football DNA Alternatives

- **England Football Learning.** The FA's [England Football Learning](https://learn.englandfootball.com/) site publishes its own session programmes and coaching articles. It's worth looking at first, because it is the governing body's own material, and it is also where the FA's coaching courses are booked, which our guide to [what qualifications you need to be a football coach](/coaching/what-qualifications-do-i-need-to-be-a-football-coach) explains.
- **Your club's coaching lead.** Many clubs share session plans between age groups. It costs nothing to ask before subscribing to anything.
- **Free drill sites.** Fine for single activities. They rarely give you a full themed session or show it being coached.

Football DNA is for planning training. For the match-day side, such as rotating players fairly and tracking minutes, [Football Parent's Coach App](/football-parent-coach-app) covers that job and can sit alongside it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Football DNA have a free trial?

No. Football DNA's terms say it does not offer a free trial. Instead, the annual plan has a 14-day money-back guarantee, and there is a free section of 56 practices and sessions you can use without paying.

### Is Football DNA on iPhone and Android?

Yes. The app is on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/football-dna/id1611898337) and [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Football.DNA), and the walkthroughs in this review were recorded on an Android phone. You can't buy a membership in the app: you sign up on the website, then log in on the app.

### Is Football DNA suitable for beginner coaches?

Yes. Beginner coaches arguably get the most from it, because the filmed sessions show how a practice is delivered as well as how it is set up. Several Trustpilot reviewers say the same.

### Is Football DNA good for under 7s?

It has content for that age group. You can filter sessions by U5 to U8, and there is a U5 to U8 curriculum, whose first block covers hiding and carrying the ball, passing and receiving, ball striking and 1v1s. Our own use was with an U8 team.

### Can a whole club use one Football DNA account?

Not one shared login. Football DNA's [club membership](https://footballdna.co.uk/club-memberships/) is for clubs with 5 or more coaches, paid as one annual fee, with a custom plan above 50 coaches. The price is on request.

### Can you cancel Football DNA at any time?

Yes. There is no minimum term: cancelling stops the next renewal, and you keep access until the end of the period you have paid for. Only the annual plan has a 14-day money-back guarantee.
