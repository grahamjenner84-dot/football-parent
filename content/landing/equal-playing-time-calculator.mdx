## The formula

Take the number of outfield positions in your format, multiply that by the length of the match, then divide by the number of outfield players you actually have available that day. That gives you target minutes per player.

## A worked example

A 7-a-side match, 50 minutes long, with a squad of 10 and one child in goal for the whole game:

| Step | Sum | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outfield positions | 7 minus the keeper | 6 |
| Total outfield minutes | 6 x 50 | 300 |
| Outfield players sharing them | 10 minus the keeper | 9 |
| Target minutes each | 300 divided by 9 | about 33 |

If your keeper rotates outfield as well, the whole squad shares both the outfield minutes and the time in goal, so the target per player rises slightly.

The number itself matters less than having one. Once you know the target, you can plan your substitutions against it instead of guessing on the touchline.

## Where a one-off sum stops working

One match is easy enough. The problem is the second match, and the ninth.

Squad numbers change every week. Someone misses two Sundays with a cold, someone else plays a full game because you were short. Uneven weeks are almost impossible to spot one at a time, and by the time a parent asks, the gap has usually been building since October.

Redoing the sum by hand every Sunday is the part nobody keeps up.

## What the Coach App does with it

You set the rule once: equal minutes for everyone, a minimum each, or a fixed lineup you adjust yourself. It applies that to every fixture without you working it out again.

Formations are drag and drop, so changing your shape reworks the rotation around it rather than starting you from scratch.

Every player's share of the available minutes updates the moment you save a match, and the season view adds the weeks up. That is the figure a parent actually wants when they ask whether their child is getting a fair go, and you can answer it from your phone.
