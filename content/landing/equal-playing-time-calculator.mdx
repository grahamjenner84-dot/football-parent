## The formula

Take the number of outfield positions in your format, multiply that by the length of the match, then divide by the number of outfield players you actually have available that day.

That gives you target minutes per player. It is the method [Sheffield FA sets out](https://sheffieldfa.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/76000060364-how-to-plan-equal-playing-time-calculating-minutes-) in its guidance, and it is the one most coaches end up at.

## A worked example

A 7-a-side match, 50 minutes long, with a squad of 10 and one child in goal for the whole game:

| Step | Sum | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outfield positions | 7 minus the keeper | 6 |
| Total outfield minutes | 6 x 50 | 300 |
| Outfield players sharing them | 10 minus the keeper | 9 |
| Target minutes each | 300 divided by 9 | about 33 |

If your keeper rotates outfield as well, the whole squad shares both the outfield minutes and the time in goal, so the target per player rises slightly.

The number itself matters less than having one. Once you know the target, you can plan your substitutions against it instead of guessing on the touchline.

## Where a one-off sum stops working

For a single match, that is genuinely all the calculator you need, and there are free ones online that will do the arithmetic for you.

The problem is the second match, and the ninth. Squad numbers change every week. Someone misses two Sundays with a cold, someone else plays a full game because you were short. Uneven weeks are almost impossible to spot one at a time, and by the time a parent asks, the gap has usually been building since October.

Redoing the sum by hand every Sunday is the part nobody keeps up.
