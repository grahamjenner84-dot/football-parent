Reusable `<ExpertOpinion>` material and a running log of pending/answered
expert-quote requests. Check "Available quotes" before asking Graham to
contact an expert for a new one — real material that already exists beats a
fresh request. Cap reuse of any single quote at **2-3 articles** so it
doesn't read as duplicated content across the site; track reuse in the
"Used in" column.

## Available quotes

### 1. Technique before tactics

> "Player development frameworks such as that used at Football DNA believe
> in a core viewpoint - technique must precede tactics. Being able to
> execute consistently high level actions should be the main priority for
> any young player, especially those aged between 5 and 12."

**Topics:** academy pathway, player development, what coaches look for at
trials, how to become a professional footballer.
**Source:** Part 1, "When should tactics be introduced?"
**Used in:** [how-to-join-a-football-academy](/academy-pathway/how-to-join-a-football-academy) (1/3)

### 2. The "Golden Age" of motor learning

> "Biology dictates that the period between 5 and 11 represents the 'Golden
> Age' of motor learning. Here, a child's brain is absorbent for physical
> skill acquisition but cognitively ill-equipped for complex, rigid tactical
> structures."

**Topics:** relative age effect, late developers, academy readiness, player
development timeline.
**Source:** Part 1, "When should tactics be introduced?"
**Used in:** [signs-your-child-is-ready-for-academy-football](/football-development/signs-your-child-is-ready-for-academy-football) (1/3)

### 3. Rigid positions suppress young players

> "I am of the strong belief that positional responsibilities, rigid team
> shapes and tactical formations should not form any part of an U7 player's
> development... forcing them to stay in a fixed position on a 5v5 pitch can
> suppress their creativity, curiosity and enjoyment of the game."

**Topics:** early-years coaching, good football development environment,
talent identification at young ages.
**Source:** Part 1, "Learning positions in 3v3."
**Used in:** (none yet)

### 4. Adults are the obstacle

> "While the FA builds frameworks to maximise a child's long-term technical
> capability, the biggest obstacle to player development remains the adults
> who find it difficult to relinquish control."

**Topics:** parent behaviour, touchline conduct, biggest football parent
mistakes, what to say after matches.
**Source:** Part 2, "Why are clubs avoiding 3v3?"
**Used in:** (none yet)

### 5. Embracing chaos builds problem-solvers

> "Far from delaying a child's ability to play as a unit, embracing the
> chaos of 3v3 forces them to discover how to work together effectively... I
> believe it will build autonomous, adaptable young players who can solve
> problems under pressure, rather than passive bystanders."

**Topics:** improve football decision-making, building confidence in young
footballers, what scouts look for (problem-solving under pressure).
**Source:** Part 2, "Is 3v3 too chaotic?"
**Used in:** (none yet)

### 6. Bigger pitches mean fewer touches and decisions

> "On larger pitches with more players, individuals typically receive less
> touches of the ball, complete fewer technical actions, make a reduced
> number of decisions and have restricted opportunities to solve problems in
> realistic game situations."

**Topics:** playing up an age group, new FA youth football format, academy
pathway age-appropriate progression.
**Source:** Part 2, "Why is 11v11 being delayed until U14?"
**Used in:** (none yet)

### Secondary voice (non-Football DNA, use sparingly)
